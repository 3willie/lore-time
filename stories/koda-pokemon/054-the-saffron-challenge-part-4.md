# The Saffron Challenge, Part Four

One Pokémon left each.

Koda looked at his belt.

Arcanine was tired from the Mr. Mime battle. Pidgeot hadn't been built for this chamber or this matchup. Dragonair had handled Sabrina's Kadabra well but Dragon type against a pure Psychic this strong —

He reached for Arcanine's ball.

Arcanine stepped out and looked at Alakazam without flinching.

Alakazam regarded Arcanine with the same total attention it had given everything else.

Sabrina spoke for the first time since the battle began in earnest.

"Fire type against Psychic. Unconventional."

"Alakazam is a thinking Pokémon," Koda said. "Arcanine is a feeling one. Sometimes feeling moves faster than thinking."

Sabrina looked at him with something that finally shifted in her expression. Not quite surprise. Closer to interest.

---

Alakazam opened with Psychic immediately — full force, no preamble.

"Arcanine — don't plan. Just move."

Arcanine launched sideways on pure instinct, the Psychic grazing his flank rather than landing clean. He kept moving — not in a pattern, not in a calculated arc, just continuous motion driven by instinct and the deep trust Koda had built into him over months.

Alakazam tracked him. Adjusted. Fired again.

Arcanine wasn't where it aimed.

*It's working. Alakazam reads intention — Arcanine isn't forming intentions fast enough to read.*

"Flamethrower — when it feels right. Don't wait for me."

He gave Arcanine the battle.

Arcanine ran a wide arc and released Flamethrower not when Koda would have called it but when his own instinct said now — and the timing was different from anything calculated, catching Alakazam mid-adjustment, landing across its left side.

Alakazam stumbled. First time it had moved its feet.

It responded with Thunder Punch — faster than its previous attacks, abandoning the psychic approach for something more direct.

It connected. Arcanine skidded hard across the chamber floor, electricity crawling through his coat.

He got up.

Koda said nothing.

Arcanine shook the static from his fur and looked at Alakazam.

Then he did something Koda hadn't seen before — he went completely still. All that continuous motion just — stopped.

Alakazam read the stillness. Prepared.

Arcanine moved.

Not Extreme Speed — something between speed and intention, a full commitment that had no calculation in it whatsoever.

Flamethrower released at point blank range before Alakazam could process that the stillness had been the move.

The hit was decisive.

Alakazam stood for a moment — both spoons lowering slowly — and then sat down with a kind of dignity that only very old and very powerful Pokémon managed even in defeat.

The chamber went quiet.
