# The Roar That Meant *Come With Me*

Koda almost walked past it.

He was cutting through the eastern woods alone — no Athena, no Meadow, just a quiet afternoon with his pack and his thoughts — when he heard it. Not a roar exactly. More like the *idea* of a roar. A deep resonance that came up through the ground before it came through the air, settling in his chest like a second heartbeat.

He stopped walking.

Through the trees, maybe thirty feet off the path, a pair of enormous amber eyes looked back at him.

Koda didn't reach for his belt. He didn't step back. He just stood there, breathing steadily, and looked at Entei the way you look at something that could level a mountain if it felt like it.

Entei stepped forward into a shaft of afternoon light.

It was massive — mane billowing like volcanic smoke, paws the size of dinner plates pressing silently into the moss. A scar crossed its left shoulder, old and pale, the kind earned long before any trainer ever walked these woods.

It regarded Koda for a long, measuring moment.

Then it sat down.

Koda blinked. Slowly, carefully, he sat down too.

---

They stayed like that for a while. The woods were quiet around them in the particular way woods go quiet when something ancient decides to rest in them.

Eventually Koda reached into his bag and pulled out a rice ball. He ate half, thought about it, and set the other half on the ground between them.

Entei looked at the rice ball.

Looked at Koda.

Ate the rice ball in one dignified bite.

Koda smiled. "Okay," he said softly. "Hi."

Entei stood, shook its great mane once, and turned to walk deeper into the trees. Then it paused and looked back over its shoulder.

Koda grabbed his bag. "Yeah, alright."

---

The path Entei led him down didn't exist on any map. It wound through cedar and stone, across a shallow river that ran oddly warm, up a ridge that smelled faintly of rain and ozone. Koda scrambled to keep up, half jogging, trying not to look like he was struggling next to something that moved like a force of nature.

He heard them before he saw them.

First — a crack of electricity so sharp it made his teeth ache. Then a sound like a river swallowing itself whole, deep and constant and somehow patient.

The ridge opened onto a wide, flat rock shelf overlooking a valley filled with evening mist. And there, in the last of the daylight, were the other two.

**Raikou** paced the far edge of the shelf — long and lean and restless, purple mane crackling with trapped lightning, tail lashing. It moved like a thunderstorm that hadn't decided where to strike yet.

**Suicune** stood perfectly still at the center, looking out over the valley. Its ribbon-like mane drifted even without wind, crystalline blue, catching light that didn't quite seem to come from the sky. It was the most serene thing Koda had ever seen and somehow that made it the most intimidating.

Entei stepped onto the shelf and made a low sound in its chest.

Both legendaries turned.

Raikou's eyes landed on Koda and every hair on his body stood up. Electricity arced between the beast's paws and the stone beneath it.

Suicune simply looked at him. Steady. Ancient. Measuring.

Koda raised one hand in a small wave. "Hey."

---

Raikou did not immediately vaporize him, which Koda chose to interpret as a warm welcome.

It circled him twice at a distance, lightning flicking off its paws with each step, then huffed a breath of charged air directly into his face and went back to pacing. Koda's hair stood on end for a solid five minutes.

Suicune approached slowly, head low, ribbons drifting behind it. It stopped close enough that Koda could see his own reflection in its deep red eyes. Then it pressed its nose briefly to his forehead — a touch cold and clean as snowmelt — and stepped back.

He had the strange, unshakeable feeling he had just been evaluated by something that had been alive since before the first human ever set foot in these woods. And that he had, narrowly, passed.

Entei settled behind him like a small volcano choosing to nap.

Koda sat down on the warm rock between the three legendary beasts as the mist filled the valley below and the stars came out one by one above them.

He didn't have his Pokédex. He didn't have his team. He had a half-empty bag and slightly singed eyebrows from Raikou's welcome.

He'd never felt so small, or so completely at ease.

---

He made it back to the path eventually, long after dark, guided by the faint glow of Raikou's lightning in the trees behind him until he hit familiar ground. When he turned to look back, there was nothing — just dark woods and the distant sound of wind.

But on the rock shelf, he knew, three ancient things were still out there. Watching the valley. Keeping whatever old agreement they kept.

Koda walked back to camp with warm stone still in his boots and something quiet and enormous sitting in his chest.

He didn't tell anyone about it for a long time.

Some things, he thought, were just for you.
