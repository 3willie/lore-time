# The Marsh Badge and What Comes After

Athena was sitting on the gym steps when Koda pushed through the doors, Eevee in her lap, journal open but not being written in. She looked up at him and read the badge case before he said a word.

"Five," she said.

"Five." He sat beside her on the steps. "Sabrina wants to battle you."

Athena looked at the gym doors.

"She said that?"

"She sensed you when we arrived. Said your ability is genuine." He paused. "Her word."

Athena was quiet for a moment — not nervous, processing. Eevee looked up at her and she scratched its ears absently.

"She's psychic," Athena said. "She already knows what I can do."

"Probably."

"That's either an advantage or a significant problem."

"Go find out."

She closed her journal, set Eevee gently beside Koda, and stood.

She looked at the doors for one more second.

Then went in.

---

Koda sat on the steps with Eevee and waited.

He let Arcanine out to rest in the afternoon sun, still tired from Alakazam. Dragonair coiled loosely on the warm stone. Kadabra sat beside him in the particular way it had developed of being present without demanding anything.

Eevee watched the gym doors with focused attention the entire time.

*It knows,* Koda thought. *It's tracking her through the walls somehow.*

---

Inside — he couldn't see it, but he pieced it together later from what Athena told him that evening.

Sabrina had met her at the chamber entrance and done something she apparently didn't do for other challengers.

She'd spoken first.

*"You don't command them. You listen to them. That's rarer than any technique."*

Athena had said — *"Is that going to make this easier or harder?"*

*"Harder,"* Sabrina had said. *"My Pokémon will trust you. They'll fight harder because of it."*

---

The battle itself ran forty minutes — longest gym battle Koda had heard of.

Athena's Eevee against Sabrina's Kadabra. Not a power matchup by any measure — but Eevee moved with that uncanny instinctive quality, the product of an animal that had been genuinely communicated with rather than trained in the traditional sense. It understood what was being asked of it at a level that bypassed normal instruction.

Kadabra read intentions. Eevee didn't form them the same way other Pokémon did — it responded to the actual moment, to what it genuinely felt from Athena, and that was harder to anticipate than calculated movement.

Athena had added a Pidgeotto she'd caught on the northern road — fast, aggressive, well-bonded. It handled Mr. Mime in a grinding aerial battle that wore both Pokémon down to a narrow finish.

Alakazam and Eevee for the final round.

Koda had asked her how she beat it.

She'd been quiet for a moment.

*"I didn't tell Eevee what to do. I just told it how I felt about it. That I trusted it completely. That whatever happened was okay."*

*"And?"*

*"Alakazam read that. And I think — I think it didn't know what to do with a Pokémon that wasn't trying to win. Just trying to be honest."*

She'd paused.

*"Eevee won. I'm still not entirely sure how."*

---

When Athena came through the gym doors the afternoon light had shifted toward gold.

She held the Marsh Badge loosely, looking at it with a slightly distant expression.

Eevee launched off the step and she caught it without looking, holding it against her chest.

Koda stood.

"Well?" he said.

"She's extraordinary," Athena said.

"The badge?"

She held it up briefly. Then pocketed it.

"She said something before I left." Athena looked at him. "She said — *the disruption to the east isn't natural and it isn't random. Something is being built. Be careful.*"

Koda went still.

"She knew about it."

"She's known for a while I think." Athena looked east — the direction she'd mentioned the night before the city. "She was waiting for someone to go look."

---

They left Saffron City the following morning heading east — a direction neither of them had originally planned, following the thread Athena had been pulling on since before they'd met on the farmland road.

The terrain east of Saffron changed quickly — city giving way to rough scrubland, the kind of in-between landscape that didn't belong fully to civilization or wilderness. Dry grass, scattered rock formations, sparse trees.

And wrong.

Koda felt it within the first hour. The air moved oddly — not naturally wrong, artificially wrong. Disrupted in patterns that didn't match wind or weather or terrain.

*Something is interfering with the air currents. Something mechanical.*

Athena walked with her attention spread wide — reading the wildlife, or the absence of it.

"There's almost nothing out here," she said quietly. "This scrubland should have Pokémon everywhere. Ekans, Sandshrew, Spearow. I'm getting almost nothing."

"They left," Koda said. "Or were pushed out."

"Both, I think."

---

They found the first evidence two hours in — a section of flattened scrubland, the grass pressed down in perfect geometric circles. Equipment marks. Recent.

Athena crouched and pressed her hand to the ground.

"Heavy machinery," she said. "And cages. Multiple."

Koda read the air above the flattened area. The disruption was stronger here — the artificial current pattern originating from further east and slightly north.

He looked at Athena.

She looked at him.

"Team Rocket," they said together.

---

They moved carefully from that point — no recalled Pokémon, everyone out and close, moving through the scrubland with the particular quiet of people who had both learned that arrival mattered as much as capability.

Koda kept the air still around them — not manipulating, just preventing. No wind signatures to give away movement through dry grass.

Athena read the landscape ahead in ways he couldn't entirely follow — pausing, redirecting, once stopping them both with a hand and waiting thirty seconds before moving again. He didn't ask why. He'd learned that her sense of the wildlife around them was as reliable as his sense of the air.

A Sandshrew appeared at the edge of a rock formation and watched Athena with frightened eyes.

She crouched immediately.

A long moment passed between them — nothing visible, just Athena present and open and whatever Athena did when she did this thing.

The Sandshrew moved closer. Stopped a few feet away.

Then turned and looked northeast deliberately.

"Thank you," Athena said softly.

She stood and looked at Koda.

"Northeast," she said. "Large structure. They've been here at least two weeks."

Koda looked northeast.

The air current disruption was strongest in that direction. Whatever they'd built was running continuously — power-hungry, large scale, not designed to be temporary.

*This isn't a harvest operation like the forest,* he thought. *This is something else.*

"We need to see what it is before we do anything," he said.

"Agreed."

They moved northeast through the scrubland, following the Sandshrew's direction and the pull of the disrupted air, toward whatever Team Rocket had been building in the empty land east of Saffron City.

The afternoon light was long and amber around them.

Neither spoke.

The scrubland stretched ahead, quiet and wrong, waiting to show them what it was hiding.
