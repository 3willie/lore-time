# The Quiet Swing

The path on the map went around the corner and stopped at the park.

Tiny letters appeared: *The Quiet Swing.*

"Let's go see," said {{hero}}.

At the park, kids were everywhere. Climbing. Sliding. Shouting.

But on the very last swing sat a girl, all by herself. She wasn't swinging. She was just watching.

"That's the quiet swing," whispered {{brother}}.

"Should we say hi?" said {{hero}}.

{{brother}} felt shy. His tummy did a little flip. But he remembered the forest of wind chimes. *You can be scared and brave.*

He walked over.

"Hi," he said. "I'm {{brother}}. Do you want to play?"

The girl looked at her shoes. "I don't know the games here," she said. "I'm new."

"We can teach you," said {{hero}}. "Or you can teach us one."

She thought about it.

"I know one," she said. "It's called Sleeping Lions. Everybody lies down very still. If you move, you're out."

"I'm good at still!" said {{brother}}.

They played Sleeping Lions on the grass. Soon, other kids came over to see. Then they played too. Seven lions. Then ten!

{{brother}} wiggled first. He was out. He didn't mind.

The girl's name was Priya. She won three times.

"Will you come tomorrow?" she asked.

"Yes!" said both boys.

That night, the map had drawn the park. And on the last swing sat a tiny girl, swinging high.

She wasn't quiet anymore.
