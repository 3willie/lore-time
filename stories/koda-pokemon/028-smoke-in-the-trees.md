# Smoke in the Trees

Koda smelled it before he saw it.

Not fire — something chemical. Sharp and artificial, the kind of smell that didn't belong in a forest. It moved against the wind, which meant it was coming from multiple directions at once.

He stopped walking.

The forest had gone quiet. No Pidgey calls. No rustling in the undergrowth. The particular silence that meant every wild Pokémon in the area had already made a decision and gone still.

*Something's wrong.*

He recalled Pidgeot from the air quietly — didn't want eyes on them from above. Kept Arcanine close. Let Kadabra drift forward slightly, scanning.

Kadabra's eyes flickered. It looked back at Koda and tilted its head left.

*That direction.*

---

He moved through the trees carefully, keeping low, reading the airflow as he went. The pressure pattern ahead was wrong — too many moving bodies in a concentrated space, the air pushed and displaced in ways that mapped out a rough picture before he saw anything.

*At least six people. Machinery. Something large being dragged.*

He crouched at the tree line and looked through.

A wide forest clearing — and Team Rocket had turned it into an operation.

Four agents in black uniforms worked a net system stretched between the trees — thick cables connected to a central winch, designed to drop and contain anything that moved through the space. Two more agents managed a set of cages along the far edge, already holding three Pokémon. A Nidoran. Two Bellsprout. All pressed to the back of their cages, frightened and still.

A seventh figure stood in the center directing everything — older than the others, sharp-eyed, a clipboard in hand like this was inventory work.

*They're running a harvest,* Koda realized. *Systematic. They've done this before.*

He looked at his team. Kept his voice barely above a breath.

"We take down the net system first. Free what's in the cages. Then we deal with the agents."

Arcanine's ears were flat and forward. Ready.

Kadabra's eyes were already cold.

Dratini had gone completely still in a way Koda recognized now — not fear. Focused. The way it looked before Dragon Rage.

Pidgeot's ball was in his hand.

*All of us.*

---

## The Net System

Koda sent Kadabra forward through the trees on the left flank — silent, floating low, invisible against the shadow.

Two agents near the winch heard nothing before Confusion lifted them both off their feet and set them firmly but harmlessly against a tree trunk, where they stayed, dazed and pinned.

The winch operator turned and shouted.

That was the signal.

"Arcanine — Flamethrower, cut the net cables!"

Arcanine burst from the tree line, low and fast, sweeping a controlled line of fire across the cable rigging. The synthetic lines caught immediately — engineered to be tough but not fireproof. The net system collapsed inward, useless.

Two agents came at Arcanine directly with their own Pokémon — an Ekans and a Koffing.

*There it is.*

"Arcanine — yours. Pidgeot —"

He released Pidgeot upward into the canopy.

"— keep Koffing away from the cages. If that thing uses Smog near the captured Pokémon—"

Pidgeot was already moving, cutting between Koffing and the cage line with a Steel Wing that sent Koffing spinning wide. It repositioned immediately, blocking the angle again.

*Good. Hold that space.*

---

## The Cages

Koda moved toward the cages himself while his team held the center of the clearing.

The supervising agent stepped into his path.

He was bigger up close. Calm in a way that suggested experience.

"Smart kid," the man said. "Wrong move though."

He released two Pokémon at once — Arbok and Weezing. Both large. Both experienced. Both positioning immediately to cut off the cage access.

Koda felt the air.

*Arbok moves in straight lines. Weezing floats — it'll try to get elevation and blanket the area.*

He needed both handled fast.

"Dratini."

Dratini slid forward from beside him — fluid, unhurried, exactly the way it moved through the stream at camp.

The agent looked at it and almost laughed. "A Dratini? That's your play?"

Dratini's eyes moved to Weezing first. Then Arbok. Then back to Weezing.

*It's already made its decision.*

"Thunder Wave — Weezing first. Ground it."

Dratini released a precise arc of static that caught Weezing mid-float. It sputtered, electricity crawling across its surface, and dropped two feet before stabilizing — grounded, sluggish, out of the elevation game.

Arbok lunged.

Dratini met it head on with a Wrap that surprised the agent visibly — Dratini moving with a speed and confidence that hadn't been there a week ago, coiling around Arbok's momentum and redirecting it into the earth cleanly.

Arbok thrashed. Dratini held.

*It's holding,* Koda noted. *A week ago it would have pulled back.*

Dragon Rage followed — point blank, decisive. Arbok went still.

Dratini released it carefully. Not cruelly.

The agent stared.

Koda was already moving past him toward the cages.

---

The locks were combination-based — no keys. Koda pressed his fingers against the first cage's mechanism and felt the air inside the lock's chamber. Tiny pressure points. Tumblers.

He let a focused thread of air move through the mechanism — delicate, careful. The kind of control that took practice to trust.

The lock clicked.

The Nidoran pressed itself to the back of the cage, still frightened.

"It's okay," Koda said quietly. "You can go."

He opened the door fully and stepped back.

Nidoran watched him for three seconds. Then bolted into the undergrowth.

The Bellsprout cages were next — same method, same result. Both disappeared into the forest immediately, there and then gone.

He heard more Pokémon crashing through the brush from the direction of the net collapse — wild ones that had been caught in the cables now freed and running.

*Good.*

---

## The Supervisor Makes a Choice

The supervising agent had watched all of it. His two Pokémon were down. His team was pinned, disoriented, or simply standing still assessing what had happened in the last four minutes.

He looked at Koda with something that wasn't quite anger.

"You're the one from Vermilion," he said. "Surge's badge."

Koda said nothing.

"You're going to be a problem," the man continued. He pulled out a radio. "Command needs to know about you."

Koda held his gaze.

"Tell them," he said simply.

The agent paused. Then — almost despite himself — a small nod. Not respect exactly. Recognition.

He recalled his Pokémon and walked east with what remained of his team, unhurried, like retreating was a tactical decision and not a defeat.

Koda watched them go.

---

## After

The clearing settled slowly. Birdsong returned first — tentative, then fuller. The chemical smell began to thin as the forest breathed it out.

Koda stood in the center of what had been a systematic operation twenty minutes ago. Cable remnants. Empty cages with their doors hanging open. Scorch marks from Arcanine's fire along the tree line — controlled, precise, nothing that wouldn't heal.

His team came back to him one by one.

Arcanine pressed against his side, warm and solid.

Pidgeot landed on a low branch and looked at the empty cages.

Kadabra floated beside him quietly.

Dratini wound slowly around his feet once — something it had never done before — and then settled.

Koda looked at each of them.

"You all made decisions out there," he said. "I barely had to ask."

He knelt and put a hand out. Dratini rested its head against his palm the same way it had on the limestone shelf by the river. Like a habit now. Like trust that had finished becoming trust.

"That was your first real fight," Koda said. "How do you feel?"

Dratini looked at him steadily.

Then looked at the open cages.

Then back at him.

*It understands,* Koda thought. *What we were doing and why.*

He stood and checked the tree line one more time. The forest was resettling around them — not quite normal yet, but moving that direction.

He looked at the map.

One more day to the next city.

"Let's keep moving," he said.

They followed him back into the trees without hesitation.

All four of them.
