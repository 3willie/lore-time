# The Road Between Storms and Stone, Part Two

## Battle Two — The Mountain Trio

Higher up the trail, where the path turned rocky, three older trainers were camped beside a boulder field. They had the look of people who'd been traveling a long time — worn boots, well-kept Poké Balls.

"We heard two trainers were coming up the south road," the tallest one said. "We've been waiting. Two on two, two separate battles. Best of two."

Their team spread across the rocks — Onix, Mankey, and Fearow.

*Terrain suits them,* Koda noted. *Rocky ground, elevated sightlines. They picked this spot on purpose.*

**Kadabra vs. Mankey**

Mankey was relentless — all speed and fury, closing distance before Kadabra could create separation. Teleport kept it safe for two exchanges, but Mankey started *predicting* the repositioning, cutting off angles.

*It's learning Kadabra's pattern.*

"Kadabra — stop teleporting. Hold ground. Confusion, sustained."

It was a risk. Mankey landed a Karate Chop before the Confusion fully built. But once it hit, the sustained pressure was too much. Mankey slowed, stumbled, and went down.

Kadabra swayed but stayed standing.

**Meadow's Geodude vs. Onix**

This one was all earth.

Onix moved through the boulder field like it was home — and it was. It used the terrain, weaving between rocks, making a clear line of attack nearly impossible.

Meadow was quiet for a long moment, watching.

Then she pressed one hand lightly to the ground.

Koda saw it — the faintest tremor moving outward from her palm, subtle enough that only he would recognize it for what it was. Onix paused, just slightly confused by the counter-vibration beneath it.

That half second was enough.

"Geodude — Rollout, follow the left channel!"

Geodude launched through the gap Meadow had sensed open, connecting squarely with Onix's mid-section. Three follow-up hits before Onix could reorient.

When it finally stopped moving, the lead trainer exhaled slowly.

"That was the best Geodude performance I've ever seen," he said, meaning it.

Meadow patted Geodude once. It leaned into her hand like a big round boulder-cat.

## That Evening — Campfire, No Battle

They stopped where the trail widened near a flat rock overhang. Koda built a small fire. Meadow didn't ask how — she just watched the way the wood caught too quickly and too evenly and filed it away without comment.

Arcanine stretched out near the heat. Bulbasaur settled against him like it was the most natural thing in the world. Pidgeot roosted above. Kadabra sat cross-legged — or the closest thing to it — eyes half closed.

"Your Kadabra is more patient than it used to be," Meadow said.

"It got there before I did," Koda admitted.

She pulled a small notebook from her bag — hand-drawn maps, notes on terrain, little sketches of her Pokémon. She'd been documenting everything.

"Do you miss home?" she asked. Not sad. Just honest.

Koda thought about it properly before answering.

"Sometimes. But I think I was supposed to end up here." He looked at Arcanine, at Kadabra, at Pidgeot shifting in the branches. "They needed me. And I needed this."

Meadow looked at Bulbasaur sleeping soundly against Arcanine's side.

"Yeah," she said quietly. "Me too."

The fire held steady between them. No wind disturbed it.
