# Wings of Fire and Lightning

The stars stretched endlessly in every direction, a black ocean salted with light. Koda floated on his back, arms wide, watching a distant nebula bloom pink and gold at the edge of the galaxy. Beside him, Rion drifted lazily, his Lapras form shimmering faintly in the starlight, flippers fanning slow circles in the void.

"I keep thinking," Koda said, "that space should feel cold. But it doesn't feel like anything."

"That's because you're not thinking about it hard enough," Rion said. "If you thought about it harder, you'd feel it."

Koda looked at him. "That is the least scientific thing anyone has ever said."

Before Rion could respond, a streak of gold and crimson split the dark like a comet — then another of electric blue and white — and suddenly the two legendary birds were simply *there*, enormous and blazing, filling the void with heat and crackling ozone.

Moltres. Zapdos.

They didn't attack. They didn't threaten. They were simply curious, the way ancient things sometimes are — tilting their massive heads, studying the two small creatures floating in their domain.

Moltres lowered its beak and, gently, almost tenderly, nipped the tip of Koda's fingers.

The fire hit him like a memory he'd never had.

It started in his hand and rolled outward — golden and roaring — and Koda had just enough time to say *oh* before his whole body became something else entirely. His arms spread wide and wider, feathers erupting in cascades of amber and deep red, each one tipped in living flame. His feet curved into talons. His voice, when it came, was not a voice at all — it was a *cry*, pure and wild and carrying across lightyears, the sound a sunset would make if it could scream.

He beat his wings once and felt the stars wobble.

Nearby, there was a crack of electricity sharp enough to split thought in half. Rion — no longer a Lapras, no longer anything so quiet — stretched yellow wings that crackled at every feather, bolts of lightning threading through his plumage like veins. His eyes, when they opened, were bright white. He let out a shriek that sent static shimmering in rings through the dark.

The two legendary birds regarded their new counterparts with something like satisfaction.

Then Moltres tucked its wings and dove — not away, but *alongside* Koda, and Koda understood without thinking. He folded his flaming wings and followed, and they fell together through the dark in spirals of fire, trailing light that would take a thousand years to reach the nearest planet.

Above, Zapdos shot sideways in a zigzag crack of thunder, and Rion matched it beat for beat, lightning answering lightning, two storms choosing each other.

They flew until the galaxy turned beneath them like a wheel.

And for two boys who had spent so long trying to figure out what they were supposed to be — it was, for a few blazing minutes, enough to simply *blaze.*
