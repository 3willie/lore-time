# Learning to Trust the Sky, Part One

The road northeast wound through tall grass and scattered woodland — good open country, the kind that gave a young Dragon Pokémon room to move without feeling cornered.

Koda had made a decision before they left the river.

Dratini needed battles. Needed to learn what it was capable of. But more than that — it needed to learn that battle didn't mean danger. Didn't mean threat. Didn't mean the kind of world it had been hiding from in that quiet pool.

That was going to take time.

That was fine.

---

## First Attempt

The first wild battle found them before Koda was ready to introduce it — a Rattata darting out of the tall grass, aggressive and quick.

Koda glanced at Dratini, moving loosely alongside him.

"Want to try?"

Dratini looked at the Rattata. Then at Koda. Then back.

It coiled slightly — not the defensive coil from the pool. Something more considered.

Koda nodded once. *Your call.*

Dratini moved forward.

Rattata lunged immediately and Dratini pulled back sharply — more startled than hurt. It hissed. Rattata pressed forward again.

*It's not used to things coming at it,* Koda noted. *Everything in that pool kept its distance.*

"You don't have to win this one," Koda said calmly. "Just stay with it."

Dratini steadied. When Rattata lunged a third time Dratini held its ground and answered with a Wrap — instinctive, clean, its body coiling around Rattata's momentum and redirecting it into the ground.

Rattata scrambled free and disappeared into the grass.

Dratini turned back to Koda. Uncertain. Had that been right?

Koda crouched to eye level.

"That was good. You read it and you moved. That's the whole thing."

Dratini held his gaze for a moment. Then looked away — but not with anxiety. More like it was processing.

*It's thinking,* Koda realized. *Already.*

---

## Second Battle — Finding Range

Two days later on a rocky hillside path, a trainer with a Geodude challenged them. Koda sent Dratini out without hesitation.

Geodude opened with Tackle — pure blunt force.

Dratini dodged left, smooth and fast. Better than last time.

"Dratini — Thunder Wave. Test the distance."

Dratini gathered the charge — it came naturally, the way water flows downhill — and released it in a short arc. Geodude shuddered, static crawling across its surface. Slowed down.

*There. That's Dratini's range.*

"Wrap. Use the slowdown."

Dratini coiled around Geodude precisely, applying pressure until Geodude conceded the match.

The trainer blinked. "Is that a Dratini? Where did you even—"

"River," Koda said simply.
