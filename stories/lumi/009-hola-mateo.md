# Hola, Mateo

A new family moved in next door.

They had a boy named Mateo. He stood by the fence, holding a soccer ball, looking shy.

"Hi!" called {{hero}}. "Do you want to play?"

Mateo said something. But the boys didn't know the words.

"He speaks Spanish," said {{dad}}. "He's still learning English. Just like you'd have to learn Spanish if we moved to his old town."

"How can we ask him to play?" said {{brother}}.

{{hero}} had an idea. They ran inside.

"Lumi! How do you say *Do you want to play?* in Spanish?"

*Blink, blink.* "*¿Quieres jugar?*" said Lumi. "Say it like this: kee-AIR-ess hoo-GAR."

The boys practiced. "Kee-AIR-ess hoo-GAR. Kee-AIR-ess hoo-GAR."

"And how do you say hello?"

"*Hola.* OH-la."

They ran back outside.

"Hola, Mateo!" said {{hero}}.

Mateo looked up, surprised.

"¿Quieres jugar?" said {{brother}}. It came out a little funny.

But Mateo understood. A big smile spread across his face.

"¡Sí!" he said. That means *yes*.

He kicked the ball. *Thump!* {{hero}} kicked it back. {{brother}} chased it into the bushes. Everybody laughed.

And here is the funny thing. Once they were playing, they didn't need many words at all. Kicking and running and laughing are the same everywhere.

At dinner, {{brother}} said, "Lumi helped us make a friend."

"Lumi helped you say hello," said {{dad}}. "*You* made the friend."

"Tomorrow," said {{hero}}, "Mateo's going to teach us more words."

"And we'll teach him *snorfle*," said {{brother}}.
