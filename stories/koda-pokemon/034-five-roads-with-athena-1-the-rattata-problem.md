# Five Roads With Athena 1: The Rattata Problem

The farmland north of the well gave way to a patchwork of small villages connected by dirt roads — the kind of place where people knew each other and strangers were noticed.

The first village they passed through had a problem.

An older farmer flagged them down at the road's edge, hat in hand, genuinely distressed. A large Rattata colony had moved into his grain storage — not one or two, a full group, eating through winter reserves faster than he could manage.

"Tried everything," he said. "Can't use traps near the animals. Can't use anything strong or the grain gets ruined."

The two boys he'd tried to hire had scared the Rattata deeper into the storage and made it worse.

Koda looked at Athena.

She was already looking at the storage barn.

---

They went in together — Koda reading the air inside, mapping movement by pressure and displacement. Athena doing something quieter, eyes moving along the walls, the floor, following whatever she followed.

"Twelve," she said after a moment. "One larger one in the back corner. That's the lead."

She crouched near the entrance and went still.

Koda felt her focus shift outward.

The rustling in the grain slowed. A small brown face appeared at the top of a grain sack — watching Athena with cautious curiosity rather than the panic that a loud approach would have triggered.

"They're not aggressive," she said quietly. "Just hungry. Bad season pushed them in."

"Can you move them?"

"I can ask."

Koda didn't smile but came close.

She spoke — not words exactly, something between tone and intention — and the rustling pattern changed. Slow at first. Then purposeful.

Rattata emerged one by one from the grain, the large one last, moving along the barn wall toward the open side door Koda had left available.

He guided a gentle current of air through the space — not pushing, just suggesting a direction. Natural. Like a breeze that happened to be helpful.

The last Rattata slipped through the door.

The farmer stood outside with his mouth open.

"How did you—"

"They needed somewhere better," Athena said. "There's a field of wild grain two hills east. They'll be fine."

She walked back to the road without further explanation.

Koda followed.

The farmer called thank you three times before they were out of earshot.
