# The Morning After, Part Two

They packed and left Saffron City by mid-morning, taking the northern road — wide and well-traveled, the kind of route that moved through proper towns rather than scrubland, civilization in both directions.

The air was cleaner than yesterday. The interference completely gone now, the natural currents restored, the wind moving through the city's edges the way wind was supposed to move.

Koda let himself feel that.

Dragonair was still moving carefully — not injured but conserving, the way smart Pokémon did after real effort. Koda kept the pace easy. No rushing.

Athena walked beside him reading the landscape in her sideways way — attention moving between the path ahead and everything peripheral. A Spearow flock overhead. A Rattata watching from a roadside wall. A Drowzee sitting in a field that she paused for, head tilted, before nodding once and continuing.

"What did it say?" Koda asked.

"That the road north is safe. And that there's good water two hours ahead."

"Drowzee told you that."

"In its way." She glanced at him. "You feel the air. I feel the animals. It's not that different."

He thought about it.

"No," he said. "It's not."

---

The good water turned out to be a wide clear stream cutting across a meadow, exactly where the Drowzee had indicated. They stopped there for an hour — more for the Pokémon than themselves.

Dragonair slid into the stream immediately and moved through it in long slow passes, the water doing something for it that rest alone couldn't. Its pearl orb brightened incrementally with each pass.

Koda watched from the bank.

*Water heals you in a way I can't quite reach,* he thought. *That's yours. I'm glad you have it.*

Eevee waded in to its knees and sat there looking deeply satisfied.

Arcanine drank at the upstream edge and then lay in the shallows — huge and apparently unbothered by being wet, eyes half closed in the morning sun.

Kadabra sat on a dry rock above the stream and observed everything with its customary expression of being the only one taking the situation seriously.

Pidgeot hunted upstream, graceful and purposeful, entirely in its element.

Athena sat beside Koda on the bank and watched all of it.

"Your team looks different than it did a week ago," she said.

"How so?"

She considered. "More settled. Like they've stopped wondering what kind of team they are and just — are it."

Koda looked at them. Arcanine and Eevee in the shallows. Dragonair moving through the deep. Kadabra on its rock. Pidgeot upstream.

Five different Pokémon from five different places. Nothing about them that should naturally cohere.

And yet.

"What about yours?" he asked.

She looked at Eevee sitting contentedly in the shallows.

"Eevee's been settled since the first day," she said. "I think it knew before I did what this was going to be."

"Pidgeotto?"

"Getting there." A pause. "It's learning that I'm not going to push it into things. That takes time to trust."

Koda nodded.

*I know something about that.*
