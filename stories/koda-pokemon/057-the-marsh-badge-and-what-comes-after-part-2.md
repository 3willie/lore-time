# The Marsh Badge and What Comes After, Part Two

The battle itself ran forty minutes — longest gym battle Koda had heard of.

Athena's Eevee against Sabrina's Kadabra. Not a power matchup by any measure — but Eevee moved with that uncanny instinctive quality, the product of an animal that had been genuinely communicated with rather than trained in the traditional sense. It understood what was being asked of it at a level that bypassed normal instruction.

Kadabra read intentions. Eevee didn't form them the same way other Pokémon did — it responded to the actual moment, to what it genuinely felt from Athena, and that was harder to anticipate than calculated movement.

Athena had added a Pidgeotto she'd caught on the northern road — fast, aggressive, well-bonded. It handled Mr. Mime in a grinding aerial battle that wore both Pokémon down to a narrow finish.

Alakazam and Eevee for the final round.

Koda had asked her how she beat it.

She'd been quiet for a moment.

*"I didn't tell Eevee what to do. I just told it how I felt about it. That I trusted it completely. That whatever happened was okay."*

*"And?"*

*"Alakazam read that. And I think — I think it didn't know what to do with a Pokémon that wasn't trying to win. Just trying to be honest."*

She'd paused.

*"Eevee won. I'm still not entirely sure how."*

---

When Athena came through the gym doors the afternoon light had shifted toward gold.

She held the Marsh Badge loosely, looking at it with a slightly distant expression.

Eevee launched off the step and she caught it without looking, holding it against her chest.

Koda stood.

"Well?" he said.

"She's extraordinary," Athena said.

"The badge?"

She held it up briefly. Then pocketed it.

"She said something before I left." Athena looked at him. "She said — *the disruption to the east isn't natural and it isn't random. Something is being built. Be careful.*"

Koda went still.

"She knew about it."

"She's known for a while I think." Athena looked east — the direction she'd mentioned the night before the city. "She was waiting for someone to go look."

---

They left Saffron City the following morning heading east — a direction neither of them had originally planned, following the thread Athena had been pulling on since before they'd met on the farmland road.

The terrain east of Saffron changed quickly — city giving way to rough scrubland, the kind of in-between landscape that didn't belong fully to civilization or wilderness. Dry grass, scattered rock formations, sparse trees.

And wrong.

Koda felt it within the first hour. The air moved oddly — not naturally wrong, artificially wrong. Disrupted in patterns that didn't match wind or weather or terrain.

*Something is interfering with the air currents. Something mechanical.*

Athena walked with her attention spread wide — reading the wildlife, or the absence of it.

"There's almost nothing out here," she said quietly. "This scrubland should have Pokémon everywhere. Ekans, Sandshrew, Spearow. I'm getting almost nothing."

"They left," Koda said. "Or were pushed out."

"Both, I think."
