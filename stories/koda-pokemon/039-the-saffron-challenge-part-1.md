# The Saffron Challenge, Part One

## Silph Co. and the Psychic Gym

Saffron City was different from every city before it.

Bigger. Denser. The buildings taller and closer together, the streets moving with a particular urban energy that made Celadon feel like a quiet village. At the center of it all — Silph Co., the massive corporate tower that dominated the skyline like a second city built on top of the first.

And at the eastern edge, quieter than its surroundings suggested it should be — the Saffron Gym.

No garden like Celadon. No industrial floor like Vermilion. Just a clean, spare building with a subtle energy that Koda felt the moment he approached the entrance. Something focused and inward, like the building itself was paying attention.

*Psychic gym,* he thought. *Everything here will read you before you move.*

He'd spent the previous day walking the city, thinking through what he knew.

Sabrina. Saffron's gym leader. Renowned as the most powerful Psychic trainer in Kanto. Her Pokémon didn't just battle — they anticipated. Predicted movement. Read intention before it became action.

*Which means reactive battling loses before it starts.*

He'd sat with that for a long time.

*If they read what I'm about to do — I have to not know what I'm about to do until the moment I do it. Instinct over plan. Trust the team.*

Athena had listened to him work through it over breakfast without interrupting. When he finished she said simply — "Your Kadabra already thinks that way. Let it lead."

He'd thought about that all morning.

---

The gym's interior was a series of interconnected rooms — a teleportation maze, warping trainers from space to space unpredictably. Disorienting by design. Koda moved through it methodically, feeling the air displacement of each teleport pad before stepping on it, mapping the system the way he mapped weather.

*Pressure. Direction. What moves where.*

The gym trainers were all psychic specialists — Abra, Kadabra, Mr. Mime, Jynx. Each battle was a lesson in how much faster a Psychic type moved when it understood your intention.

Koda gave his team one consistent instruction throughout.

"Don't telegraph. Just move."

Kadabra thrived under it — fighting mirror matches against opposing Kadabra with an intuitive fluidity that surprised even Koda. Dragonair handled the Mr. Mime battles, Dragon Rage cutting through the barrier constructs cleanly. Arcanine read the Jynx matchup on its own and handled it with careful fire work and relentless mobility.

By the time the maze delivered him to the central chamber, Koda felt like the gym itself had been a training session.

Sabrina was waiting.

She stood at the far end of the chamber — tall, still, dark eyes that felt like they were already three moves into a conversation you hadn't started yet. No expression exactly. Just complete attention.

She looked at Koda for a moment that stretched slightly longer than normal.

"You don't decide what you're going to do until you do it," she said. Not impressed. Observing.

"I'm working on it," Koda said.

"It's not a technique," she said. "It's a state of mind. There's a difference."

She released her first Pokémon.

Kadabra — hers, fully evolved, carrying the particular stillness of a Pokémon that had been trained by a psychic master for years.

Koda looked at his own team.

He reached for Dragonair's ball.

---

Dragonair emerged and immediately felt the opposing Kadabra's presence — that focused psychic weight that filled the chamber like a second atmosphere.

It didn't react. Just settled into itself.

*Good,* Koda thought.

Sabrina's Kadabra opened with Psybeam — not fast, almost leisurely, aimed with surgical precision at exactly where Dragonair's head was.

Dragonair moved. Not dramatically. Just — wasn't there.

Koda hadn't said anything.

*It felt the intention before the move. It's doing what I asked without being asked.*

"Thunder Wave," Koda said.

Dragonair released the arc. Kadabra teleported — reappeared behind Dragonair, Psychic building in its eyes.

"Wrap."

Dragonair turned into the attack rather than away — coiling toward Kadabra's position in the moment between the Psychic's build and release, getting inside the range where the attack lost focus.

The Psychic discharged wide.

Dragonair held Kadabra in a firm Wrap.

Dragon Rage at close range.

Sabrina's Kadabra went down cleanly.

Sabrina watched without visible reaction. Recalled it.

Released her second Pokémon.

Mr. Mime.

It constructed a barrier immediately — layered, dense, the kind that turned the battlefield into a series of invisible walls that redirected attacks unpredictably.

Koda looked at Dragonair — tiring from the Kadabra battle.

"Good work. Come back."

He released Arcanine.

Mr. Mime's barrier system was designed to frustrate direct attackers — Flamethrower hit a barrier and scattered sideways, Extreme Speed redirected off invisible walls and lost momentum.

*It's controlling the geometry of the whole space.*

Koda felt the air in the chamber. The barriers displaced it — he could feel their positions the same way he felt weather systems.

*There. The gaps.*

"Arcanine — Flamethrower, angle thirty degrees left of center."

Arcanine adjusted without hesitation.

The Flamethrower threaded through a barrier gap and caught Mr. Mime directly.

Mr. Mime reconstructed immediately — new barrier geometry, gaps relocated.

Koda felt them shift.

"Twenty degrees right. High."

Another thread. Another hit.

Mr. Mime was taking damage faster than it could manage its own system. It faltered — barriers thinning, gaps widening.

"Extreme Speed. Straight through."

Arcanine launched into the weakened barrier wall and broke through it entirely, the impact on Mr. Mime decisive.

Mr. Mime sat down.

Sabrina recalled it.

One left.

She reached for her final ball with the same unhurried calm she'd had throughout — and released Alakazam.

The chamber changed.

Not visibly. But the psychic presence that had been significant before became something else entirely — deeper, older, the kind of focused intelligence that made the air feel different.

Alakazam looked at Koda directly.

Not at Arcanine. At Koda.

Koda held its gaze.

*It's reading me. Right now. Everything.*

He took a breath and let his mind go quiet — not planning, not calculating. Just present. The way air was present. The way weather simply was.

He reached for Kadabra's ball.
