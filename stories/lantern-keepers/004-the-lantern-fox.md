# The Lantern Fox

Something was stealing the cookies.

Every morning, the tin in Old Wick's kitchen was a little emptier. Crumbs led out of the door, down the rocks, and vanished.

"A thief," {{brother}} said darkly. "We have to catch it."

So that night, the brothers hid behind the rain barrels with a flashlight and a plate of cookies as bait. They waited — {{brother}} was very good at waiting now — until a small shape slipped out of the shadows.

It was a fox. A little red fox, no bigger than a cat. And the tip of its tail was glowing, soft and gold, like a candle that had learned to walk.

It took one cookie, very politely, and looked straight at them.

Then it trotted away up the path toward the tower.

"Follow it," {{hero}} whispered.

The fox led them up the curling stairs, past the green lamp and the blue lamp and the little silver one, all the way to the top. It stopped beside the Great Lamp, the one that had been dark for so long, and sat down.

Then it touched its glowing tail to the glass.

For one heartbeat, the Great Lamp flickered — a warm golden shimmer, deep inside. Then it faded.

"Ember," said a voice behind them. Old Wick stood in the doorway in his slippers. "So that's where you've been."

"You *know* him?" {{brother}} gasped.

"Ember was the last keeper's fox," said Old Wick softly. "He's been waiting a long time for someone to light that lamp again."

"But how?" {{hero}} asked. "What does it light for?"

Old Wick looked at the two boys, standing side by side.

"The Great Lamp is too big for one keeper," he said. "It lights for two. Two people who trust each other completely. The last keepers were sisters. When one of them left, it went dark."

{{hero}} looked at {{brother}}. {{brother}} looked at {{hero}}.

Outside, far away over the sea, a long line of black clouds was gathering. Thunder rumbled, low and slow.

"Storm's coming," said Old Wick. "The biggest one of the summer. Three days."

Ember's tail glowed a little brighter.

{{hero}} took a deep breath. "Then we've got three days to learn how."
