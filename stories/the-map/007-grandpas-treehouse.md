# Grandpa's Treehouse

This time, the map drew a place for {{dad}}.

On the way home, {{dad}} had stopped to help an old man fix a flat tire. In the rain! He got all wet. He didn't make a fuss about it.

"Anyone would help," he said.

That night, the map drew a tall tree with a little wooden house in its branches.

*The Treehouse of Summer.*

"Dad has to come!" said {{brother}}. "It's *his* place!"

{{dad}} laughed. But he looked at the map for a long time.

That night, three hands touched the map together. *Swoosh!*

The treehouse was real. It had a rope ladder and a soft, mossy floor. Outside, it was a warm summer evening, with fireflies blinking in the grass. *Blink, blink.*

On the wall, lots of names were carved into the wood. Some were very old.

{{dad}} touched them. Then he stopped.

There, near the bottom, were Grandpa's letters. And right next to them were some small, wobbly letters.

{{dad}}'s letters.

"I was here," said {{dad}} quietly. "When I was little. With Grandpa. I forgot!"

"Maybe grown-ups just get busy," said {{hero}}.

{{dad}} smiled, but his eyes were a little wet. "Grandpa used to say, *the map draws what you earn. So keep being kind.*"

"You helped that man with his tire," said {{brother}}. "That was kind."

{{dad}} hugged them both, tight.

Then, very carefully, under his own letters, he helped the boys add theirs. One for {{hero}}. One for {{brother}}.

The next morning, {{dad}} made pancakes. For no reason at all. And he hummed the whole time.
