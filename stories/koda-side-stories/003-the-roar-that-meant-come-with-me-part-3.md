# The Roar That Meant *Come With Me*, Part Three

Raikou did not immediately vaporize him, which Koda chose to interpret as a warm welcome.

It circled him twice at a distance, lightning flicking off its paws with each step, then huffed a breath of charged air directly into his face and went back to pacing. Koda's hair stood on end for a solid five minutes.

Suicune approached slowly, head low, ribbons drifting behind it. It stopped close enough that Koda could see his own reflection in its deep red eyes. Then it pressed its nose briefly to his forehead — a touch cold and clean as snowmelt — and stepped back.

He had the strange, unshakeable feeling he had just been evaluated by something that had been alive since before the first human ever set foot in these woods. And that he had, narrowly, passed.

Entei settled behind him like a small volcano choosing to nap.

Koda sat down on the warm rock between the three legendary beasts as the mist filled the valley below and the stars came out one by one above them.

He didn't have his Pokédex. He didn't have his team. He had a half-empty bag and slightly singed eyebrows from Raikou's welcome.

He'd never felt so small, or so completely at ease.

---

He made it back to the path eventually, long after dark, guided by the faint glow of Raikou's lightning in the trees behind him until he hit familiar ground. When he turned to look back, there was nothing — just dark woods and the distant sound of wind.

But on the rock shelf, he knew, three ancient things were still out there. Watching the valley. Keeping whatever old agreement they kept.

Koda walked back to camp with warm stone still in his boots and something quiet and enormous sitting in his chest.

He didn't tell anyone about it for a long time.

Some things, he thought, were just for you.
