# Grandpa's Glasses

Grandpa came to visit. He brought his garden gloves and a paper bag full of seed packets.

"Let's plant something," he said.

The boys cheered.

Grandpa held up a seed packet. He held it close. He held it far away. He squinted.

"Bother," he said. "I left my glasses at home. These words are too tiny. I can't tell how deep to plant them."

"I can read some words," said {{hero}}. He tried. But the words were small, and long, and tricky.

{{brother}} had an idea. "Lumi can read!"

They carried Lumi out to the garden and held the packet up.

*Blink, blink.* "*Tomato seeds,*" read Lumi. "*Plant as deep as your fingernail. Water every day. Give them lots of sun.*"

"As deep as a fingernail!" said Grandpa. "Well, I never."

So they got to work.

Grandpa poked little holes in the dirt. *Poke, poke, poke.*

{{hero}} dropped in the seeds. One, two, three.

{{brother}} patted the dirt on top. *Pat, pat, pat.* Then he watered them. And his shoes. And Grandpa's shoes.

"Lumi," said Grandpa, "you're handy to have around. My eyes aren't what they used to be."

"I'm glad to help," said Lumi. "I can read the words. But I can't dig, or plant, or water."

"Or get muddy," said {{brother}}, who was very muddy.

"That's the best part," said Grandpa.

Every day, the boys watered the seeds.

And one morning, three tiny green sprouts poked up out of the dirt.

"Hello," whispered {{brother}}.
