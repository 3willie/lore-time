# The Forest of Wind Chimes

The forest sang.

Every tree was hung with wind chimes — glass ones and wooden ones, shell ones and silver ones — and when the breeze moved, the whole forest rang like a thousand tiny bells.

"It's beautiful," {{brother}} whispered.

Then they heard the other sound. Under all the music, very small, someone was crying.

They found her curled up at the foot of an old oak: a tiny creature with big ears and soft gray fur, no bigger than a mouse. She was holding a chime made of three little acorns. It wasn't making any sound at all.

"It's mine," she sniffed. "Every creature in the forest has a chime. When you hang it up, the wind carries your worry away. But mine won't ring. My worry is too big."

"What's your worry?" {{hero}} asked gently.

She looked at the ground. "I'm scared of the dark. Everyone else here is brave. If they find out, they'll laugh."

{{hero}} knelt down. He didn't say anything for a moment. Then he said something he had never told anyone, not even {{brother}}.

"I'm scared of the dark too," he said. "I'm the big brother, so I pretend I'm not. But sometimes I leave the hall light on and say it's for {{brother}}. It's not. It's for me."

{{brother}} stared at him. "Really?"

"Really."

{{brother}} took his hand. "That's okay," he said. "I'm scared of thunder. And bees. And the noise the bath makes."

The little creature looked from one to the other, her ears slowly lifting.

"You're scared," she said. "And you're still here. In a forest. At night."

"Yeah," said {{hero}}. "Turns out you can be both."

She stood up. She reached up on tiptoe and hung her acorn chime on the lowest branch of the oak.

The wind came. And for the first time, her chime rang — a soft, round, wooden sound, like a laugh.

All around them, the forest answered.

When the boys woke up in their beds, the hall light was still on. {{hero}} didn't switch it off. He didn't need to explain it anymore.

On the map, a tiny acorn chime hung from a tiny oak. And far away, at the very edge of the paper, something new was beginning to draw itself — rooftops and towers, and the faint outline of birds with clockwork wings.
