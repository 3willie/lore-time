# The Road Between Storms and Stone

They didn't make a formal decision. Meadow simply fell into step beside him as he stood to leave, and Koda didn't question it. Some things didn't need to be said out loud.

Arcanine walked between them. Bulbasaur rode on Meadow's shoulder like it had always been there.

---

## Battle One — The Riverside Twins

Two siblings — a girl and a boy, maybe nine and eleven — were training near a shallow river crossing when they spotted Koda and Meadow.

"Double battle!" the older one called out. "We've been practicing!"

Their Pokémon were Psyduck and Poliwag. Energetic. Eager.

Koda and Meadow exchanged a glance.

"Arcanine and Bulbasaur," Koda said.

Meadow nodded. "Keep it grounded."

Psyduck opened with Confusion, targeting Arcanine. Poliwag sent a rolling Water Gun sweeping wide, trying to split them apart.

Arcanine took the Confusion hit and steadied. Bulbasaur's vines shot low and wrapped Poliwag before it could redirect the water stream.

"Arcanine — Ember, gentle. Don't overwhelm."

A controlled burst pushed Psyduck back without hurting it badly. Koda kept his wind entirely still — this was Arcanine's moment to read the field.

Bulbasaur used Vine Whip twice in quick succession, patient and precise, until Poliwag sat down exhausted.

Psyduck lasted a little longer — headache working against it as much as anything — before Arcanine's final Flamethrower ended it cleanly.

The siblings cheered anyway. Good sports.

"You two move like you've been training together for years," the older one said.

Meadow glanced at Koda. "Something like that."

---

## Battle Two — The Mountain Trio

Higher up the trail, where the path turned rocky, three older trainers were camped beside a boulder field. They had the look of people who'd been traveling a long time — worn boots, well-kept Poké Balls.

"We heard two trainers were coming up the south road," the tallest one said. "We've been waiting. Two on two, two separate battles. Best of two."

Their team spread across the rocks — Onix, Mankey, and Fearow.

*Terrain suits them,* Koda noted. *Rocky ground, elevated sightlines. They picked this spot on purpose.*

**Kadabra vs. Mankey**

Mankey was relentless — all speed and fury, closing distance before Kadabra could create separation. Teleport kept it safe for two exchanges, but Mankey started *predicting* the repositioning, cutting off angles.

*It's learning Kadabra's pattern.*

"Kadabra — stop teleporting. Hold ground. Confusion, sustained."

It was a risk. Mankey landed a Karate Chop before the Confusion fully built. But once it hit, the sustained pressure was too much. Mankey slowed, stumbled, and went down.

Kadabra swayed but stayed standing.

**Meadow's Geodude vs. Onix**

This one was all earth.

Onix moved through the boulder field like it was home — and it was. It used the terrain, weaving between rocks, making a clear line of attack nearly impossible.

Meadow was quiet for a long moment, watching.

Then she pressed one hand lightly to the ground.

Koda saw it — the faintest tremor moving outward from her palm, subtle enough that only he would recognize it for what it was. Onix paused, just slightly confused by the counter-vibration beneath it.

That half second was enough.

"Geodude — Rollout, follow the left channel!"

Geodude launched through the gap Meadow had sensed open, connecting squarely with Onix's mid-section. Three follow-up hits before Onix could reorient.

When it finally stopped moving, the lead trainer exhaled slowly.

"That was the best Geodude performance I've ever seen," he said, meaning it.

Meadow patted Geodude once. It leaned into her hand like a big round boulder-cat.

---

## That Evening — Campfire, No Battle

They stopped where the trail widened near a flat rock overhang. Koda built a small fire. Meadow didn't ask how — she just watched the way the wood caught too quickly and too evenly and filed it away without comment.

Arcanine stretched out near the heat. Bulbasaur settled against him like it was the most natural thing in the world. Pidgeot roosted above. Kadabra sat cross-legged — or the closest thing to it — eyes half closed.

"Your Kadabra is more patient than it used to be," Meadow said.

"It got there before I did," Koda admitted.

She pulled a small notebook from her bag — hand-drawn maps, notes on terrain, little sketches of her Pokémon. She'd been documenting everything.

"Do you miss home?" she asked. Not sad. Just honest.

Koda thought about it properly before answering.

"Sometimes. But I think I was supposed to end up here." He looked at Arcanine, at Kadabra, at Pidgeot shifting in the branches. "They needed me. And I needed this."

Meadow looked at Bulbasaur sleeping soundly against Arcanine's side.

"Yeah," she said quietly. "Me too."

The fire held steady between them. No wind disturbed it.

---

## Battle Three — Morning Ambush

They were barely an hour back on the trail when four trainers stepped out from both sides — a planned double ambush, grinning like they'd been waiting all morning.

"Heard you two are good," the lead one said. "Prove it. Two on two, simultaneous."

Four battles at once — two per side.

*Chaotic,* Koda thought. *Good practice.*

He looked at Meadow.

She looked back.

No words needed.

**Koda — Pidgeot vs. Fearow**

A sky battle. Pure speed, pure instinct. Fearow was experienced — drilling fast, changing angles mid-dive.

Koda watched the airflow. *There.* A slight pressure difference where Fearow committed too hard on a left bank.

"Pidgeot — wait for the overcorrection. Then Steel Wing."

Pidgeot held, absorbed a Peck hit without breaking, then launched off the impact's momentum — Steel Wing catching Fearow completely off-guard.

One clean hit. Fearow down.

**Meadow — Bulbasaur vs. Sandshrew**

Sandshrew dug immediately, vanishing underground.

Meadow crouched. Fingers to the soil.

She tracked it.

"Left of the second bush," she said quietly. "Now."

Bulbasaur drove vines directly into the ground at that exact point. Sandshrew burst back up, disoriented, and a follow-up Tackle ended it.

The ambush trainers stared.

"How did she know where it was?" one whispered.

Koda smiled and kept walking.

---

By the time Vermilion City's gate came into view that afternoon, their Pokémon had stopped separating by trainer. Arcanine walked next to Bulbasaur. Geodude rolled alongside Kadabra. Pidgeot circled wide enough to watch all of them.

"They're becoming one team," Meadow observed.

"Yeah." Koda watched Arcanine nudge Bulbasaur gently with his nose. "They are."

She adjusted the strap on her bag.

"I'm not going for the Vermilion badge," she said. "My path goes a different direction for a while."

Koda nodded. He'd half expected it.

"Then we split here."

"For now," she said. Not a goodbye. A *see you later* wrapped in two words.

Bulbasaur looked up at Arcanine for a long moment. Then trotted back to Meadow's side.

Arcanine watched it go, then pressed his head briefly against Koda's shoulder.

Koda scratched behind his ears.

"I know," he said. "Me too."

He watched Meadow take the eastern fork without looking back — steady, unhurried, exactly like herself — until the tree line swallowed her up.

Then he turned toward Vermilion City.

Third badge waiting.
