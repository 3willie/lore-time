# Smoke in the Trees, Part Three

## The Supervisor Makes a Choice

The supervising agent had watched all of it. His two Pokémon were down. His team was pinned, disoriented, or simply standing still assessing what had happened in the last four minutes.

He looked at Koda with something that wasn't quite anger.

"You're the one from Vermilion," he said. "Surge's badge."

Koda said nothing.

"You're going to be a problem," the man continued. He pulled out a radio. "Command needs to know about you."

Koda held his gaze.

"Tell them," he said simply.

The agent paused. Then — almost despite himself — a small nod. Not respect exactly. Recognition.

He recalled his Pokémon and walked east with what remained of his team, unhurried, like retreating was a tactical decision and not a defeat.

Koda watched them go.

---

## After

The clearing settled slowly. Birdsong returned first — tentative, then fuller. The chemical smell began to thin as the forest breathed it out.

Koda stood in the center of what had been a systematic operation twenty minutes ago. Cable remnants. Empty cages with their doors hanging open. Scorch marks from Arcanine's fire along the tree line — controlled, precise, nothing that wouldn't heal.

His team came back to him one by one.

Arcanine pressed against his side, warm and solid.

Pidgeot landed on a low branch and looked at the empty cages.

Kadabra floated beside him quietly.

Dratini wound slowly around his feet once — something it had never done before — and then settled.

Koda looked at each of them.

"You all made decisions out there," he said. "I barely had to ask."

He knelt and put a hand out. Dratini rested its head against his palm the same way it had on the limestone shelf by the river. Like a habit now. Like trust that had finished becoming trust.

"That was your first real fight," Koda said. "How do you feel?"

Dratini looked at him steadily.

Then looked at the open cages.

Then back at him.

*It understands,* Koda thought. *What we were doing and why.*

He stood and checked the tree line one more time. The forest was resettling around them — not quite normal yet, but moving that direction.

He looked at the map.

One more day to the next city.

"Let's keep moving," he said.

They followed him back into the trees without hesitation.

All four of them.
