# Smoke in the Trees, Part Two

## The Cages

Koda moved toward the cages himself while his team held the center of the clearing.

The supervising agent stepped into his path.

He was bigger up close. Calm in a way that suggested experience.

"Smart kid," the man said. "Wrong move though."

He released two Pokémon at once — Arbok and Weezing. Both large. Both experienced. Both positioning immediately to cut off the cage access.

Koda felt the air.

*Arbok moves in straight lines. Weezing floats — it'll try to get elevation and blanket the area.*

He needed both handled fast.

"Dratini."

Dratini slid forward from beside him — fluid, unhurried, exactly the way it moved through the stream at camp.

The agent looked at it and almost laughed. "A Dratini? That's your play?"

Dratini's eyes moved to Weezing first. Then Arbok. Then back to Weezing.

*It's already made its decision.*

"Thunder Wave — Weezing first. Ground it."

Dratini released a precise arc of static that caught Weezing mid-float. It sputtered, electricity crawling across its surface, and dropped two feet before stabilizing — grounded, sluggish, out of the elevation game.

Arbok lunged.

Dratini met it head on with a Wrap that surprised the agent visibly — Dratini moving with a speed and confidence that hadn't been there a week ago, coiling around Arbok's momentum and redirecting it into the earth cleanly.

Arbok thrashed. Dratini held.

*It's holding,* Koda noted. *A week ago it would have pulled back.*

Dragon Rage followed — point blank, decisive. Arbok went still.

Dratini released it carefully. Not cruelly.

The agent stared.

Koda was already moving past him toward the cages.

---

The locks were combination-based — no keys. Koda pressed his fingers against the first cage's mechanism and felt the air inside the lock's chamber. Tiny pressure points. Tumblers.

He let a focused thread of air move through the mechanism — delicate, careful. The kind of control that took practice to trust.

The lock clicked.

The Nidoran pressed itself to the back of the cage, still frightened.

"It's okay," Koda said quietly. "You can go."

He opened the door fully and stepped back.

Nidoran watched him for three seconds. Then bolted into the undergrowth.

The Bellsprout cages were next — same method, same result. Both disappeared into the forest immediately, there and then gone.

He heard more Pokémon crashing through the brush from the direction of the net collapse — wild ones that had been caught in the cables now freed and running.

*Good.*
