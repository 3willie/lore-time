# Sharp and Steady, Part Three

## Evening — All of Them

The last battle of the day came from a trainer who'd apparently been watching them work through the afternoon from a distance. She had a full team and wanted a rotation battle — one at a time, switching allowed, his four against her three.

It was the most complex battle of the day.

Kadabra and Arcanine opened. Her Alakazam and Machamp answered — a matchup designed to split the team's strengths, Machamp pressuring Kadabra physically while Alakazam handled Arcanine's fire with psychic redirection.

Koda rotated Dragonair in for Kadabra — the physical resistance buying time while Arcanine found Alakazam's new positioning.

Pidgeot came in and out twice — disrupting, repositioning, never staying long enough to become a target but changing the battlefield geometry each time it moved through.

Dragonair anchored the finish — outlasting her final Pokémon through patience and efficient energy use, Dragon Rage landing only when the timing was right rather than whenever the charge was ready.

Her team was good. His team was better — not in power but in coordination. In the particular way four different Pokémon had stopped needing instruction for individual moments and started reading the whole battle as a shared thing.

She recalled her last Pokémon and stood quietly for a moment.

"How long have you been training together?" she asked.

Koda thought about the first morning on the road out of Pallet. Kadabra as an Abra in a Poké Ball he'd just earned. Growlithe before Arcanine. Pidgeot as a wild shape banking hard through clouds.

Dratini at the edge of a pool, hissing, coiled tight against the world.

"Not that long," he said. "It just — happened fast."

She shook her head slowly. "Whatever you're doing — keep doing it."

---

That night Koda sat at the edge of camp while the fire held and the team settled around it.

He looked at each of them in the firelight.

Kadabra — sharper and less cautious than it had been that morning. Arcanine — carrying new information from Rapidash in the way it held its weight. Pidgeot — already asleep, content in the high branches, a Pokémon that had found its thinking in the middle of a battle today and knew it. Dragonair — glowing faintly, processing, integrating everything the day had sent at it.

One day of battles.

All of them different than they'd been at sunrise.

Koda put more wood on the fire and leaned back against his pack.

*Sharp and steady,* he thought.

*That's the whole thing.*
