# The Good Question

{{brother}} flopped onto a kitchen chair.

"Lumi," he said. "Tell me stuff."

*Blink, blink.* "What kind of stuff?" asked Lumi.

"Just… stuff!"

"Hmm," said Lumi. "Stuff about socks? Stuff about soup? Stuff about the moon? There's so much stuff. I don't know where to start."

{{brother}} huffed. "Lumi isn't working."

{{hero}} came over. "I think you have to ask a real question," he said. "Watch."

He thought for a moment. Outside, the rain had stopped, and there were colors in the sky.

"Lumi," said {{hero}}, "why do rainbows come after rain?"

*Blink, blink.* "Good question!" said Lumi. "After rain, tiny drops of water float in the air. When sunshine goes through them, the light spreads out into colors. Red, orange, yellow, green, blue, and purple."

"Ohhh," said {{brother}}.

Now he wanted a turn. He scrunched up his face and thought.

"Lumi! Why… um… why do… worms?"

"That's nearly a question," said Lumi kindly. "What do you want to know about worms?"

{{brother}} thought some more.

"Why do worms come out when it rains?"

"*That's* a good question," said Lumi. "Worms like wet ground. When it rains, they can wiggle a long way without drying out."

"I asked a good question!" shouted {{brother}}.

"You did," said Lumi. "And you know what makes a question even better?"

"What?"

"A *please*."

{{brother}} giggled. "Lumi, why is the sky blue, *please*?"

And Lumi told him.

All afternoon, the boys asked good questions. The better the question, the better the answer.
