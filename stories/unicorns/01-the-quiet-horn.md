# The Quiet Horn

In the Glimmerwood, every unicorn's horn could do something special. Sunny's horn made rainbows. Bramble's horn made flowers bloom. Old Willow's horn could light up the whole forest like the moon.

But little Pip's horn didn't do anything at all.

"Maybe it's broken," Pip sighed, looking at her reflection in the stream.

"Horns don't break," said Willow kindly. "Sometimes they're just waiting for the right moment."

Pip didn't believe her. At the Rainbow Festival, all the young unicorns showed off their magic. Sunny painted the sky. Bramble made a carpet of daisies. Pip stood at the back and hoped nobody would ask her to try.

Then the clouds came.

They rolled in dark and heavy, and the wind blew so hard that the smallest foal, Button, got lost in the trees. Everyone called for her, but the storm was too loud.

Pip went very still. Her horn began to feel warm — not bright, not sparkly, just warm, like a hug.

She followed the feeling. It pulled her left, then right, past the big oak, down the hill… and there was Button, shivering under a fern.

"How did you find me?" Button sniffled.

"My horn knew," Pip said, surprised.

Willow smiled when they came home. "Your horn finds those who are lost, Pip. That's a quiet kind of magic. But it might be the most important kind."

That night, Pip looked at her reflection again. Her horn didn't glow or sparkle.

But she didn't feel broken anymore.
