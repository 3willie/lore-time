# Best Day at PokePark Kanto

Koda pressed his nose to the monorail window as PokePark Kanto rose out of the trees — towers shaped like Poliwag shells, a Ferris wheel with Clefairy faces on every car. "Are we there, are we there?" he asked, for the ninth time.

"Almost, buddy," Will said, hoisting Wren higher on his hip. Wren, barely two, was more interested in chewing his own sock than the theme park.

Karen had the map already unfolded before the train even stopped. "First stop: Meadow Zone. There's a Bulbasaur garden."

Koda didn't wait for a plan. The second the doors hissed open, he was gone — small sneakers slapping the cobblestones toward a cluster of Bulbasaur dozing in a sunbeam. One lifted its head, blinked its big amber eyes, and ambled over to sniff his outstretched hand. Koda went very still, the way Will had taught him. "Gentle," he whispered to himself. The Bulbasaur nudged his palm and let out a soft, pleased grumble.

"He's a natural," Karen murmured, snapping a photo.

They spent the morning drifting between zones — Koda shrieking with delight on the Magnemite swing ride, Wren finally waking up for the water splash show and shrieking too, mostly because Koda was shrieking. Will bought them both cotton candy shaped like Vulpix tails, which Koda ate with total seriousness, like it was a very important job.

By afternoon the sun was low and gold over the park, and they found a bench near the lake where trainers let their Pokémon swim. A Lapras glided by, and a family of ducklings — real ones, not Psyduck — paddled after it. Koda leaned into Will's side, sugar-sticky and sleepy.

"Best day," Koda mumbled.

"Yeah?" Will said, smiling down at him.

"Best day," Koda said again, already half-asleep, one hand still curled like he could feel the Bulbasaur's nose in it.

Karen snapped one more photo before they packed up — Wren drooling on Will's shoulder, Koda dreaming of grass-type friends, the lake turning pink with the sunset behind them.
