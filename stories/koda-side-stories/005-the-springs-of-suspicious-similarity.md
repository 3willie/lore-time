# The Springs of Suspicious Similarity

The brochure had promised *"a peaceful, rejuvenating experience in naturally heated mineral waters, surrounded by the beauty of the wilderness."*

It had not mentioned the other guests.

Koda found the hot springs just after noon, following a hand-painted sign nailed crookedly to a pine tree at the end of a dirt path. He'd packed light — a towel, a change of clothes, a sandwich he'd already eaten — and was fully prepared to spend several hours doing absolutely nothing, which he considered a serious and underappreciated skill.

He pushed through a curtain of hanging ferns and stopped.

The springs were beautiful. Steam curled off four wide pools of glittering blue-green water, ringed by smooth grey stones and ancient trees. Wildflowers grew in the cracks. A small waterfall trickled peacefully into the far pool.

Also, there were approximately thirty identical purple blobs already soaking in them.

Koda blinked.

One of the blobs turned to look at him. It had a small, flat face — two dots for eyes, a simple curved line of a mouth — and it waved a stubby little appendage.

"...Hi," said Koda.

"Hi," said the blob.

"Hi," said another blob, from the pool on the left.

"Hi," said several more blobs, in staggered sequence, like a very slow wave at a sporting event.

Koda looked down at the brochure. He looked back up. He was a reasonable person who had seen many strange things, and he decided that today was a day off, and strange things were not his problem today.

He changed, folded his clothes neatly on a dry rock, and stepped into the nearest pool.

The water was perfect. Deeply, profoundly, almost insultingly perfect — hot enough to melt tension he hadn't known he was carrying, mineral-rich in a way that made his whole body feel like it was being thanked personally. He sank in up to his shoulders and exhaled a breath that felt three days long.

A Ditto floated past him on its back.

Koda closed his eyes.

For about four minutes, it was the most relaxed he'd ever been.

"You look tired," said a voice beside him.

He opened one eye. A Ditto was sitting on the rock next to the pool, regarding him with its permanent, placid smile.

"I was relaxing," Koda said.

"Very tired," the Ditto agreed, nodding seriously.

"Thank you."

He closed his eye again. Thirty seconds passed.

"Are you a trainer?"

Koda opened both eyes. A different Ditto — or possibly the same one, there was truly no way to know — was now in the pool with him, approximately one foot away.

"Not today," he said.

"Smart," said the Ditto.

"Thank you."

Another thirty seconds.

"I could be your friend," offered a Ditto from across the pool.

"I could *also* be your friend," said another, closer.

"I was going to be his friend *first*," said a third, in a tone that was remarkably petty for a creature with no visible ears.

Koda sank lower until the water was at his chin.

In the far pool, two Ditto appeared to be copying each other in an infinite loop — one would wobble left, the other would wobble left, the first would wobble right in response, the second would wobble right — and they had clearly been doing this for some time and found it very satisfying.

One Ditto near the waterfall had transformed into a smaller, rounder version of Koda and was sitting very still, watching him, as if studying for an exam.

"Please don't," Koda said.

The Mini-Koda tilted its head.

"I mean it."

The Mini-Koda tilted its head the other way.

Koda looked up at the sky, which was a clear and innocent blue, and thought about the hot springs brochure, which had promised peace, and thought about the person who had written that brochure and what they had possibly been thinking when they wrote it.

Then he leaned his head back against the smooth stone edge of the pool.

The water was still perfect. The steam still rose in soft curls. The trees still rustled in a warm breeze.

A Ditto floated gently into his peripheral vision and just hovered there, content.

"Fine," Koda said quietly. "Fine."

He closed his eyes.

It wasn't the relaxing day he'd planned. But the water was genuinely excellent, and somewhere across the pool, two Ditto were still wobbling at each other with what could only be described as joy.

He stayed for three more hours.
