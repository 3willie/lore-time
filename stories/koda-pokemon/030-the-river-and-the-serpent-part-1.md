# The River and the Serpent, Part One

The road east out of Vermilion City followed the coastline before curving inland toward a wide river system — the kind of place that showed up on maps with small blue lines that turned out, in person, to be something much larger and older feeling.

Koda wasn't in a hurry.

Three badges felt good. Felt right. But he'd learned early that rushing toward the next thing meant missing what was already in front of you.

He let Arcanine walk loose beside him. Kadabra drifted. Pidgeot was somewhere overhead, a slow moving speck against the afternoon clouds.

Then the air changed.

Not danger — something else. A pressure near the water. Concentrated and coiled, like a storm that hadn't decided to move yet.

Koda stopped walking.

---

The river bent sharply around a limestone shelf, creating a deep still pool partially hidden by overhanging roots and tall grass. Koda pushed through carefully, Arcanine pressing close behind him.

In the center of the pool, half submerged, was a Dratini.

Small. Pale blue. Ancient-looking despite its size, the way all Dragon Pokémon carried something older than themselves in their eyes.

It saw Koda immediately.

The reaction was instant — a sharp hiss, body coiling tight, the water around it beginning to swirl in a slow warning circle.

Koda held up a hand behind him. Arcanine stopped. Kadabra stilled. No sudden movements. No Poké Balls.

Koda sat down on the limestone shelf.

Just sat. Legs crossed. Hands open on his knees.

Dratini watched him with sharp, wary eyes — still coiled, still circling the water slowly. Waiting for the trick. Waiting for the net or the ball or the lunge.

It didn't come.

---

A minute passed. Then another.

Koda didn't speak. Didn't reach for anything. He just felt the air over the pool — cool and damp, carrying the particular stillness that deep water holds even on warm days.

He let a small breath of wind move across the surface. Not directed at Dratini. Just present. Natural. The way a breeze moves because it moves, not because it was sent anywhere.

Dratini's coil loosened slightly.

It was watching the air now, not just him.

Arcanine lowered himself quietly to the ground a few feet back without being asked. Kadabra sat. Pidgeot glided in from somewhere above and landed in the branches overhead — no sudden drop, just a slow easy descent that said *we're not here to chase you.*

Dratini's eyes moved across all of them.

Still wary. But thinking now instead of reacting.
