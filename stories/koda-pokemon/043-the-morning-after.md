# The Morning After

They made it back to Saffron City well after dark.

No celebration. No debrief. Just two tired trainers and five tired Pokémon finding the Pokémon Center and letting Nurse Joy do her work without asking questions about the condition of the team.

Koda sat in the waiting area while his team recovered. Athena sat beside him, journal open, writing steadily. Processing the way she processed — through documentation, through getting it out of her head and onto paper.

He didn't talk. She didn't ask him to.

When the team was cleared he took them to the small courtyard behind the center and let everyone out in the cool night air. Dragonair moved slowly — the Electrode containment had cost it. He sat beside it for a long time, one hand resting on its neck, not talking. Just present.

Dragonair's pearl orb glowed faintly in the dark.

Still here. Still whole.

*I know,* Koda thought. *Me too.*

---

Morning came gray and soft.

Koda was already outside when Athena found him — sitting on the courtyard bench with Arcanine beside him, watching the city wake up. She had two cups of something warm from the center's small kitchen and handed him one without comment.

They sat together for a while.

"The compound will be empty when Rocket comes back to check it," she said eventually.

"They'll build somewhere else."

"Yes." She looked at her cup. "But not there. And not those Pokémon."

He nodded.

That was what it was. Not a final answer. A specific thing that had been wrong and was now less wrong. He'd learned to hold that without needing it to be more.

---

Eevee appeared in the courtyard doorway looking between them with the focused intention of a Pokémon that had decided something needed to happen.

It trotted to Athena. Sat. Looked at her.

Then looked at Koda.

Then back at her.

"I know," Athena said.

Koda looked at her.

"It thinks we've been sitting still long enough," she said.

Arcanine lifted his head from the bench beside Koda and looked at him with the same energy.

"Apparently they agree," Koda said.

---

They packed and left Saffron City by mid-morning, taking the northern road — wide and well-traveled, the kind of route that moved through proper towns rather than scrubland, civilization in both directions.

The air was cleaner than yesterday. The interference completely gone now, the natural currents restored, the wind moving through the city's edges the way wind was supposed to move.

Koda let himself feel that.

Dragonair was still moving carefully — not injured but conserving, the way smart Pokémon did after real effort. Koda kept the pace easy. No rushing.

Athena walked beside him reading the landscape in her sideways way — attention moving between the path ahead and everything peripheral. A Spearow flock overhead. A Rattata watching from a roadside wall. A Drowzee sitting in a field that she paused for, head tilted, before nodding once and continuing.

"What did it say?" Koda asked.

"That the road north is safe. And that there's good water two hours ahead."

"Drowzee told you that."

"In its way." She glanced at him. "You feel the air. I feel the animals. It's not that different."

He thought about it.

"No," he said. "It's not."

---

The good water turned out to be a wide clear stream cutting across a meadow, exactly where the Drowzee had indicated. They stopped there for an hour — more for the Pokémon than themselves.

Dragonair slid into the stream immediately and moved through it in long slow passes, the water doing something for it that rest alone couldn't. Its pearl orb brightened incrementally with each pass.

Koda watched from the bank.

*Water heals you in a way I can't quite reach,* he thought. *That's yours. I'm glad you have it.*

Eevee waded in to its knees and sat there looking deeply satisfied.

Arcanine drank at the upstream edge and then lay in the shallows — huge and apparently unbothered by being wet, eyes half closed in the morning sun.

Kadabra sat on a dry rock above the stream and observed everything with its customary expression of being the only one taking the situation seriously.

Pidgeot hunted upstream, graceful and purposeful, entirely in its element.

Athena sat beside Koda on the bank and watched all of it.

"Your team looks different than it did a week ago," she said.

"How so?"

She considered. "More settled. Like they've stopped wondering what kind of team they are and just — are it."

Koda looked at them. Arcanine and Eevee in the shallows. Dragonair moving through the deep. Kadabra on its rock. Pidgeot upstream.

Five different Pokémon from five different places. Nothing about them that should naturally cohere.

And yet.

"What about yours?" he asked.

She looked at Eevee sitting contentedly in the shallows.

"Eevee's been settled since the first day," she said. "I think it knew before I did what this was going to be."

"Pidgeotto?"

"Getting there." A pause. "It's learning that I'm not going to push it into things. That takes time to trust."

Koda nodded.

*I know something about that.*

---

They were back on the road by early afternoon, the stream meadow behind them, the northern route opening up ahead into longer views and wider sky.

Dragonair moved with its full fluidity again — the stream had done its work. It glided alongside Koda with the easy presence it had developed, close without crowding, aware of everything.

The road curved north and slightly west, following the natural geography toward the next stretch of their journey.

Koda checked his map briefly.

Two routes north. Another city. Another gym eventually.

He put the map away.

"You're not planning ahead," Athena observed.

"I'm thinking about the next step," he said. "Not the one after."

She was quiet for a moment.

"Meadow would approve of that."

Koda smiled — small and genuine.

"Where do you think she is?"

Athena looked out across the wide landscape thoughtfully.

"Somewhere with good soil and difficult terrain," she said. "Completely at home."

Koda laughed.

It was the first real laugh since before the compound. It felt like the air after the storm had cleared — clean and right and exactly what the moment needed.

Dragonair looked up at him.

He looked back at it.

*Yeah,* he thought. *That's better.*

The road stretched north ahead of them, wide and open, carrying them forward into whatever came next.
