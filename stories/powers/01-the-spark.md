# The Spark

Max was the first to notice. He was reaching for his toothbrush when — *zap!* — a tiny blue spark jumped from his fingertip to the tap.

"Did you see that?" he whispered.

His little brother Leo nodded so hard his hair bounced. "Do it again!"

Max tried. He squeezed his eyes shut and pointed at the tap. Nothing. He wiggled his fingers. Nothing. He grunted like a weightlifter. Still nothing.

"Maybe it only works when you're not trying," said Leo.

That night, Max couldn't sleep. What if the spark came back while he was in bed? What if it zapped his pillow? What if it zapped *Leo*?

In the morning, he told Dad everything. Dad listened, then sat down on the edge of the bath.

"Sounds like something big is starting," Dad said. "And big things can feel scary. But do you know what makes a power safe?"

Max shook his head.

"Learning it slowly. Being gentle. And never using it to hurt anyone."

So Max practised. Not by squeezing and grunting, but by breathing slowly, the way he did when he was nervous before swimming. In… and out. In… and out.

On the third breath, a spark danced on his fingertip — small, blue and calm. It didn't jump. It just glowed, like a firefly that had decided to stay.

Leo gasped. "It's *beautiful*."

Max smiled. The spark was not scary at all. It was his.

"Tomorrow," said Leo, "I want to find out if I have one too."

---

Max didn't know it yet, but Leo was right to wonder.
