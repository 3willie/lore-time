# The Road After Rain

They left Celadon City in the early afternoon, the sky still carrying the last of a morning rain — air clean and sharp, puddles catching light along the stone path heading north.

Koda hadn't planned the direction specifically. Just away from the city and into whatever came next.

Dragonair moved beside him on the open road — not recalled, not trailing behind. Beside. Like it had decided that was its place now and the matter was settled.

---

An hour out, the path crossed a wide meadow where three younger trainers were practicing together, running their Pokémon through drills without battling anyone specific. Just working.

One of them — a girl with a Poliwag and an eager expression — spotted Koda's team and went wide-eyed at Dragonair.

"Is that — can we battle? Please?"

Koda looked at his team. Everyone was rested. The day was long.

"One each," he said.

---

Kadabra handled the first battle without being asked to do anything extraordinary — a Drowzee that thought it could out-psychic a Kadabra. It could not. Clean and efficient, Kadabra dispatched it with the quiet confidence of a Pokémon that had stopped needing to prove itself.

Pidgeot took the second — a Doduo that was all speed and no strategy. Pidgeot let it exhaust its burst energy in two passes, then Steel Wing ended it simply.

The girl with Poliwag looked at Dragonair hopefully.

Dragonair was already moving forward.

Koda said nothing. Just watched.

Poliwag opened with Water Gun — good instincts, aiming for distance. Dragonair moved through it like water wasn't an inconvenience, which for a Dragon type it largely wasn't. Dragonair's Thunder Wave grounded Poliwag's mobility and a single Wrap ended it gently — firm but careful, the way Dragonair handled everything now. Like it understood its own size and used it accordingly.

The girl recalled Poliwag and looked at Dragonair for a long moment.

"It's so calm," she said.

"It always was," Koda said. "It just had to figure out that was okay."

---

They made camp early where the meadow met a line of birch trees, the white bark catching the last hour of afternoon light. Koda built the fire and let everyone out — the nightly ritual that had become as natural as breathing.

Arcanine settled immediately. Kadabra found a flat rock and sat. Pidgeot took the high branches.

Dragonair moved to the meadow's edge and stayed there in the low light, its pearl orb glowing faintly in the dusk — not restless, just present in the way large graceful things are present. Taking up space quietly.

Koda watched it from the fire.

Four badges. Five Pokémon becoming a real team. A journey that kept opening into something larger than the last thing it had been.

He looked up through the birch branches at the first stars appearing.

Somewhere ahead — more gyms. More road. Whatever Team Rocket was planning. Meadow on her own path somewhere. Athena too, probably.

All of it waiting.

He put another small branch on the fire and let it be enough for now — just the light, just the team, just the clean air after rain.

Dragonair turned from the meadow's edge and glided back toward the fire.

It curled near Arcanine without ceremony and closed its eyes.

Arcanine didn't move. Just exhaled slowly.

*Same,* Koda thought.

He pulled his jacket closer and watched the fire hold steady against the dark.
