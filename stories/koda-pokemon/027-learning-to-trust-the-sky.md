# Learning to Trust the Sky

The road northeast wound through tall grass and scattered woodland — good open country, the kind that gave a young Dragon Pokémon room to move without feeling cornered.

Koda had made a decision before they left the river.

Dratini needed battles. Needed to learn what it was capable of. But more than that — it needed to learn that battle didn't mean danger. Didn't mean threat. Didn't mean the kind of world it had been hiding from in that quiet pool.

That was going to take time.

That was fine.

---

## First Attempt

The first wild battle found them before Koda was ready to introduce it — a Rattata darting out of the tall grass, aggressive and quick.

Koda glanced at Dratini, moving loosely alongside him.

"Want to try?"

Dratini looked at the Rattata. Then at Koda. Then back.

It coiled slightly — not the defensive coil from the pool. Something more considered.

Koda nodded once. *Your call.*

Dratini moved forward.

Rattata lunged immediately and Dratini pulled back sharply — more startled than hurt. It hissed. Rattata pressed forward again.

*It's not used to things coming at it,* Koda noted. *Everything in that pool kept its distance.*

"You don't have to win this one," Koda said calmly. "Just stay with it."

Dratini steadied. When Rattata lunged a third time Dratini held its ground and answered with a Wrap — instinctive, clean, its body coiling around Rattata's momentum and redirecting it into the ground.

Rattata scrambled free and disappeared into the grass.

Dratini turned back to Koda. Uncertain. Had that been right?

Koda crouched to eye level.

"That was good. You read it and you moved. That's the whole thing."

Dratini held his gaze for a moment. Then looked away — but not with anxiety. More like it was processing.

*It's thinking,* Koda realized. *Already.*

---

## Second Battle — Finding Range

Two days later on a rocky hillside path, a trainer with a Geodude challenged them. Koda sent Dratini out without hesitation.

Geodude opened with Tackle — pure blunt force.

Dratini dodged left, smooth and fast. Better than last time.

"Dratini — Thunder Wave. Test the distance."

Dratini gathered the charge — it came naturally, the way water flows downhill — and released it in a short arc. Geodude shuddered, static crawling across its surface. Slowed down.

*There. That's Dratini's range.*

"Wrap. Use the slowdown."

Dratini coiled around Geodude precisely, applying pressure until Geodude conceded the match.

The trainer blinked. "Is that a Dratini? Where did you even—"

"River," Koda said simply.

---

## Third Battle — The Hard One

A trainer near a small bridge had a Spearow — fast, aggressive, all beak and noise. It came in immediately, not giving Dratini time to settle.

Spearow's first Peck landed before Dratini could track it. The second hit harder. Dratini recoiled and looked back at Koda with something complicated in its expression.

Not fear. More like — *this doesn't feel worth it.*

Koda felt the pull to intervene. Felt the air in his hands, ready.

He kept it still.

"I know," he said quietly. "It's fast. You're faster than you think."

Dratini turned back. Spearow was already diving again.

This time Dratini didn't pull back — it dropped low and let Spearow pass over it, then came up underneath with a Slam that connected solidly with Spearow's chest mid-dive.

Spearow hit the ground and didn't immediately get up.

Dratini looked at its own body — surprised at what it had just done.

Koda exhaled slowly.

*It trusted itself.*

---

## Camp — The Quiet Part

That night Koda made camp beside a wide stream. He let all four Pokémon out — something he'd started doing every evening, giving them space to just exist together without the structure of travel or battle.

Arcanine had taken to Dratini the same way he took to most things — warmly, patiently, without making a big event of it. He'd simply started walking closer to Dratini during the days, and Dratini had stopped moving away.

Kadabra observed Dratini with quiet interest, the way it observed most things — not warmly exactly, but with genuine attention. Respectful.

Pidgeot and Dratini had developed something unexpected. They'd circle each other in the evenings — Pidgeot in the air, Dratini moving through the stream shallows below — tracking each other in lazy parallel loops. Neither close nor distant. Just aware.

*They understand each other,* Koda thought. *Different elements, same kind of movement.*

He watched Dratini glide through the shallow water, unhurried, the stream parting around it naturally.

It stopped near the bank and looked up at him.

"You're getting there," Koda said.

Dratini held his gaze a beat longer than it used to.

---

## Fourth Battle — Something New

The following afternoon brought a trainer with a Slowpoke — patient, heavy, almost impossible to pressure.

Dratini tried Wrap twice. Slowpoke barely noticed.

Thunder Wave landed but Slowpoke was so naturally slow the paralysis barely changed anything. Dratini circled, frustrated, looking for an angle.

Koda watched without speaking.

*Figure it out. You can.*

Dratini shifted — something changing in its approach. It stopped trying to overpower and started moving differently. Looser. More lateral. Probing instead of committing.

Then it reared back and unleashed a Dragon Rage — not something Koda had seen from it before. A focused burst of deep blue-white energy that hit Slowpoke with enough force to end the battle cleanly.

The trainer stared. "Did — was that Dragon Rage?"

Koda stood very still for a moment.

*When did it learn that?*

He looked at Dratini. Dratini looked back with an expression that was almost — pleased with itself.

Koda laughed. Quiet and genuine.

"You've been holding out on me."

Dratini turned away like it hadn't done anything unusual and started moving down the trail.

Arcanine looked at Koda.

Koda shook his head and followed.

---

By the end of the week, Dratini moved differently than it had at the river. Not harder or faster — just more present. More willing.

It still kept something held back. Something private. The way all Dragon Pokémon seemed to carry a deeper self that they only revealed slowly, on their own terms.

Koda respected that completely.

*We're not done learning each other,* he thought. *Not even close.*

He checked the map. The next city was still two days out.

He put it away and kept walking.

Four Pokémon. All of them growing.

All of them his.
