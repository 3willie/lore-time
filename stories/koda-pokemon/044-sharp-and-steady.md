# Sharp and Steady

The route north of Saffron had no shortage of trainers.

It was one of the more traveled roads in Kanto — connecting major cities, used by trainers at every level heading in both directions. Which meant battles were available the way water was available near a river. Constantly, and without much searching.

Koda decided to use every one of them.

Not for badges. Not for a destination.

Just sharpening.

---

## Morning — Kadabra

The first trainer appeared before the city was fully out of sight behind them — a boy with a Scyther and a confident stance that said he'd won enough battles to expect winning.

Koda sent Kadabra.

Scyther was fast — genuinely fast, the kind that made most opponents reactive before they'd formed a thought. It opened with Slash, closing distance in a single movement.

Kadabra teleported.

Scyther adjusted mid-swing — better reflexes than expected — and caught Kadabra's new position on the follow-through.

Kadabra took the hit and didn't teleport away from it. Held the position. Fired Psybeam at contact range.

The exchange was close and immediate, none of the distance management Kadabra usually preferred. Koda had been deliberate about this — Kadabra's tendency to create separation was good strategy but it was also a habit. Habits became predictable.

*Learn to fight in the space you don't prefer.*

Scyther pressed again. Kadabra met it again — Disable landing on the third exchange, locking Scyther's Slash, and then the methodical Psybeam work that Kadabra did better than almost anything finishing the battle cleanly.

The boy recalled Scyther and nodded with genuine respect.

"Your Kadabra doesn't run," he said.

"It used to," Koda said. "It's learning."

---

## Midmorning — Pidgeot

A trainer with a Magneton and an Electrode — specifically chosen to challenge flying types, she said cheerfully, like she'd been waiting for a Pidgeot to come along.

She wasn't wrong. Magneton's Thunder disrupted Pidgeot's aerial mobility, forcing lower altitude. Electrode's speed on the ground cut off landing options.

Pidgeot's first two passes came up short — disrupted, redirected, unable to build the momentum it liked.

Koda watched without calling adjustments.

*Find it yourself. You know what to do when you stop trying to do what you always do.*

Pidgeot climbed instead of diving — up and above the Thunder's effective range, using the altitude not for speed but for positioning. From above, the geometry of Magneton and Electrode's coverage had gaps.

It found one.

Steel Wing through the gap, Magneton down.

Electrode tried to follow up immediately but Pidgeot was already climbing again — patient now, circling, waiting for the next gap to appear rather than forcing one.

It appeared.

Second Steel Wing landed clean.

The trainer recalled both Pokémon and looked at the sky where Pidgeot was still circling, unhurried.

"It changed strategy mid-battle," she said.

"It's been doing that more," Koda said. "I'm trying to get out of its way enough to let it."

---

## Noon — Arcanine

They stopped briefly at a waystation to eat, and a trainer eating lunch on the opposite bench looked at Arcanine with the particular expression of someone who had a Pokémon they believed could match him.

He had a Rapidash.

Speed versus speed. Fire versus fire. Both Pokémon regarding each other with the mutual acknowledgment of well-matched opponents.

It was the best battle of the morning.

Rapidash opened with Agility — already fast, now faster, moving in wide burning arcs that made tracking difficult. Arcanine met the speed with his own, Extreme Speed keeping pace, neither establishing clear advantage.

Koda let it run.

*Don't touch this one. This is Arcanine learning what fast actually means when it's coming at him.*

Five exchanges. Six. Both Pokémon reading each other in real time, adjusting, pushing.

On the seventh exchange Arcanine did something new — instead of matching Rapidash's arc, he cut across it. Shorter path, different angle, Flamethrower arriving before Rapidash expected contact.

It was the kind of move that only appeared after six exchanges of pure information gathering.

Rapidash slowed. One more Flamethrower.

Done.

The trainer looked at his Rapidash with fondness and something like pride even in defeat.

"Rapidash taught your Arcanine something," he said.

"Arcanine teaches itself," Koda said. "It just needed the right sparring partner."

The trainer smiled. "Buy it lunch then."

---

## Afternoon — Dragonair

Dragonair had three battles through the afternoon stretch — a Haunter, a Poliwrath, and a trainer with two Pokémon who wanted a doubles match and agreed to face Dragonair alone when Koda explained what he was working on.

The Haunter battle was about frustration management. Haunter's incorporeal nature made physical contact unreliable, and Dragonair's instinct when attacks passed through was to escalate — Dragon Rage building faster than necessary, energy spending inefficiently.

Koda pulled it back each time.

"Slower. Find the pattern first."

By the third attempt Dragonair was reading Haunter's materialization timing and placing Thunder Wave precisely in the moment of solidity. Once grounded and material, Haunter's advantages collapsed.

The Poliwrath battle was different — pure physical strength, close quarters, the kind of opponent that wanted to absorb and overpower. Dragonair wrapped and was countered. Wrapped again and was countered again.

It shifted.

Dragon Rage from distance, not contact. Using its superior range rather than fighting in Poliwrath's preferred space.

*There,* Koda thought. *You figured out what the battle actually was.*

The doubles trainer sent his two Pokémon and watched with interest as Dragonair managed two simultaneous opponents — not perfectly, taking more hits than in a single battle, but learning the constant position awareness that two-front fighting required.

It was messy. It was good.

---

## Evening — All of Them

The last battle of the day came from a trainer who'd apparently been watching them work through the afternoon from a distance. She had a full team and wanted a rotation battle — one at a time, switching allowed, his four against her three.

It was the most complex battle of the day.

Kadabra and Arcanine opened. Her Alakazam and Machamp answered — a matchup designed to split the team's strengths, Machamp pressuring Kadabra physically while Alakazam handled Arcanine's fire with psychic redirection.

Koda rotated Dragonair in for Kadabra — the physical resistance buying time while Arcanine found Alakazam's new positioning.

Pidgeot came in and out twice — disrupting, repositioning, never staying long enough to become a target but changing the battlefield geometry each time it moved through.

Dragonair anchored the finish — outlasting her final Pokémon through patience and efficient energy use, Dragon Rage landing only when the timing was right rather than whenever the charge was ready.

Her team was good. His team was better — not in power but in coordination. In the particular way four different Pokémon had stopped needing instruction for individual moments and started reading the whole battle as a shared thing.

She recalled her last Pokémon and stood quietly for a moment.

"How long have you been training together?" she asked.

Koda thought about the first morning on the road out of Pallet. Kadabra as an Abra in a Poké Ball he'd just earned. Growlithe before Arcanine. Pidgeot as a wild shape banking hard through clouds.

Dratini at the edge of a pool, hissing, coiled tight against the world.

"Not that long," he said. "It just — happened fast."

She shook her head slowly. "Whatever you're doing — keep doing it."

---

That night Koda sat at the edge of camp while the fire held and the team settled around it.

He looked at each of them in the firelight.

Kadabra — sharper and less cautious than it had been that morning. Arcanine — carrying new information from Rapidash in the way it held its weight. Pidgeot — already asleep, content in the high branches, a Pokémon that had found its thinking in the middle of a battle today and knew it. Dragonair — glowing faintly, processing, integrating everything the day had sent at it.

One day of battles.

All of them different than they'd been at sunrise.

Koda put more wood on the fire and leaned back against his pack.

*Sharp and steady,* he thought.

*That's the whole thing.*
