# A Place for Grandma

Grandma came to visit. She brought a pie.

After dinner, the boys showed her Grandpa's map.

"Oh," said Grandma softly. She touched the paper. "I haven't seen this in so long."

She looked at the library under the lake. The wind chime forest. The clockwork city. The treehouse.

"Did you go to these places with Grandpa?" asked {{brother}}.

"Some of them," said Grandma. She smiled a faraway smile. "He always found the best ones."

Then her smile went a little sad.

"I miss him," she said.

{{brother}} climbed onto her lap. {{hero}} sat close.

"Tell us about him," said {{hero}}.

So Grandma did.

She told them Grandpa used to whistle when he was happy. He couldn't whistle very well. It sounded like a teakettle.

She told them he grew giant pumpkins. One was so big, they needed a wagon to carry it.

She told them he always gave the last cookie away.

The boys laughed. Grandma laughed too, and wiped her eyes.

"It's nice to remember him," she said. "It's like he's here a little bit."

That night — *scritch, scratch* — the map began to draw.

Not far away. Right next to their house. A small garden, full of flowers. And in the middle, the biggest pumpkin the boys had ever seen.

*Grandma's Garden*, said the tiny letters.

In the morning, they showed her.

Grandma held the map close.

"That's our old garden," she whispered. "Exactly how it was."

{{brother}} tried to whistle.

It sounded like a teakettle.

Grandma laughed until she cried. The happy kind.
