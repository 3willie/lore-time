# The River and the Serpent, Part Two

Koda reached slowly into his bag and found a small handful of Pokémon food — nothing special, just what he carried for his own team. He set it on the limestone edge of the pool without extending his hand toward Dratini. Just placed it there and moved his hand back.

Dratini stared at it.

Didn't move.

Koda wasn't surprised. He looked away — out across the river, past the bend, toward the tree line on the far bank. Giving Dratini the space of not being watched.

He heard the faintest ripple.

Didn't look.

Another ripple. Closer.

The food was gone when he glanced back a few minutes later. Dratini had retreated to the center of the pool again, but its coil was loose now. Resting instead of ready.

*There,* Koda thought.

---

He came back the next morning.

Sat in the same spot. Same position. Brought more food and placed it at the water's edge.

This time Dratini took it while he was still watching — slowly, eyes never fully leaving him, but taking it.

Koda let the air shift again. That same gentle movement across the water's surface, unhurried.

Dratini lifted its head into it briefly. Just for a second.

*You feel that too,* Koda thought. *You understand air and water the same way I do.*

---

On the third morning Dratini was already at the pool's edge when Koda arrived.

Waiting.

Not friendly — not yet. But present. Choosing to be there.

Koda sat down in his usual spot. Set the food down. And this time, after Dratini ate, he let his hand rest on the limestone between them — open, palm up, not reaching forward.

Dratini looked at the hand for a long time.

Then, so slowly it barely seemed to happen, it moved close enough to rest its head against his palm for just a moment.

Cool and smooth. A quiet kind of trust that had been earned one morning at a time.

Koda didn't move. Didn't speak.

He felt the air settle around them both like it was exhaling.

---

He didn't throw a Poké Ball that day either.

He did the following morning — placed it on the limestone and let Dratini make the choice. It circled the ball twice, nudged it once with its nose, and then pressed against it deliberately.

One click. Two. Three.

Still.

Koda picked it up and held it carefully in both hands.

"Nobody hurt you here," he said quietly. "Nobody's going to."

He stood and looked back at Arcanine, Kadabra, and Pidgeot — all three watching patiently from where they'd waited all morning.

Arcanine's tail moved once. Slow and easy.

*Welcome,* it seemed to say.

Koda clipped the Poké Ball gently to his belt alongside the others and looked out at the river one last time. The pool was still and calm, the way it had been before they'd found each other.

He turned east and started walking.

Four Pokémon now.

And the one he'd just earned had never once been asked to fight for it.
