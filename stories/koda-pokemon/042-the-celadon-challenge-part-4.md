# The Celadon Challenge, Part Four

The glow didn't fade.

It built instead — starting at Dratini's tail and moving forward in a slow wave, the blue-white light shifting warmer, filling out, the shape changing.

Koda stood very still.

Erika took a quiet step back.

The light expanded, filled the platform, held — and then released.

Dragonair stood where Dratini had been.

Longer. More fluid. A ribbon of blue and white that moved like water even standing still, two small wings at its neck, a gentle glow still fading from its pearl-white orb.

It turned and looked at Koda.

Same eyes. Same steadiness. But deeper now — something that had been waiting inside the Dratini had finally arrived.

Koda exhaled slowly.

He walked to the edge of the platform and crouched to eye level, the way he always had at the river.

"There you are," he said quietly.

Dragonair held his gaze for a long moment.

Then it moved forward and rested its head against his hand.

---

Erika crossed the platform and stood beside him, looking at Dragonair with something genuine on her face.

"It evolved in the middle of a battle it was losing," she said.

"It decided it wasn't done," Koda said.

She was quiet for a moment. Then she reached into her jacket and held out the Rainbow Badge — small, prismatic, catching the greenhouse light in seven directions at once.

"Your Dragonair trusted you enough to push past its limit," she said. "That doesn't happen by accident."

Koda took the badge and looked at it briefly.

Then he looked back at Dragonair — still glowing faintly, still watching him, the pollen completely cleared from the air around it as if its evolution had simply decided that wasn't acceptable anymore.

He put the badge in his case alongside the others.

Four badges.

And a Dragon Pokémon that had chosen, in the hardest moment of its hardest battle, to become more.

Koda thought about the river. The limestone shelf. The food left at the water's edge three mornings in a row.

*You were always going to be this,* he thought. *You just needed somewhere safe enough to get there.*

He stood and turned toward the gym exit, Dragonair moving alongside him — fluid and quiet and entirely itself.

Outside, the Celadon City sky was clearing.
