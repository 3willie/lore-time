# Sharp and Steady, Part Two

## Noon — Arcanine

They stopped briefly at a waystation to eat, and a trainer eating lunch on the opposite bench looked at Arcanine with the particular expression of someone who had a Pokémon they believed could match him.

He had a Rapidash.

Speed versus speed. Fire versus fire. Both Pokémon regarding each other with the mutual acknowledgment of well-matched opponents.

It was the best battle of the morning.

Rapidash opened with Agility — already fast, now faster, moving in wide burning arcs that made tracking difficult. Arcanine met the speed with his own, Extreme Speed keeping pace, neither establishing clear advantage.

Koda let it run.

*Don't touch this one. This is Arcanine learning what fast actually means when it's coming at him.*

Five exchanges. Six. Both Pokémon reading each other in real time, adjusting, pushing.

On the seventh exchange Arcanine did something new — instead of matching Rapidash's arc, he cut across it. Shorter path, different angle, Flamethrower arriving before Rapidash expected contact.

It was the kind of move that only appeared after six exchanges of pure information gathering.

Rapidash slowed. One more Flamethrower.

Done.

The trainer looked at his Rapidash with fondness and something like pride even in defeat.

"Rapidash taught your Arcanine something," he said.

"Arcanine teaches itself," Koda said. "It just needed the right sparring partner."

The trainer smiled. "Buy it lunch then."

## Afternoon — Dragonair

Dragonair had three battles through the afternoon stretch — a Haunter, a Poliwrath, and a trainer with two Pokémon who wanted a doubles match and agreed to face Dragonair alone when Koda explained what he was working on.

The Haunter battle was about frustration management. Haunter's incorporeal nature made physical contact unreliable, and Dragonair's instinct when attacks passed through was to escalate — Dragon Rage building faster than necessary, energy spending inefficiently.

Koda pulled it back each time.

"Slower. Find the pattern first."

By the third attempt Dragonair was reading Haunter's materialization timing and placing Thunder Wave precisely in the moment of solidity. Once grounded and material, Haunter's advantages collapsed.

The Poliwrath battle was different — pure physical strength, close quarters, the kind of opponent that wanted to absorb and overpower. Dragonair wrapped and was countered. Wrapped again and was countered again.

It shifted.

Dragon Rage from distance, not contact. Using its superior range rather than fighting in Poliwrath's preferred space.

*There,* Koda thought. *You figured out what the battle actually was.*

The doubles trainer sent his two Pokémon and watched with interest as Dragonair managed two simultaneous opponents — not perfectly, taking more hits than in a single battle, but learning the constant position awareness that two-front fighting required.

It was messy. It was good.
