# The Other Side

Every night, the boys turned the map over to look at the back.

The little path curled out of their gate. It went along the sidewalk. And one night, it stopped at a small house with a round window.

Tiny letters appeared: *The Garden Next Door.*

"That's Mrs. Okafor's house!" said {{brother}}.

"But that's a *real* place," said {{hero}}. "Not a magic one."

They touched it together.

Nothing happened. No *swoosh*. No spinning.

"Maybe," said {{hero}} slowly, "we're supposed to walk."

So the next morning, they did. {{dad}} came too.

Mrs. Okafor was in her garden, looking at a big pile of flower pots.

"Hello, boys," she said. "My knees are sore today. I can't bend down to plant my sunflowers."

"We can bend!" said {{brother}}. He showed her. He touched his toes.

So they helped.

{{hero}} dug the holes. *Scoop, scoop.*

{{brother}} dropped in the seeds. *Plop, plop.*

Mrs. Okafor told them where each one should go. And she told them stories about when *she* was little, and climbed mango trees.

"Mango trees!" said {{brother}}. "Here?"

"Far away," she laughed. "Across the sea."

Afterward, there was lemonade. And cookies shaped like stars.

That night, the boys looked at the map.

In the garden next door, a row of tiny sunflowers had drawn itself. And the path kept going, around the corner.

"The front of the map was for magic places," said {{hero}}.

"And the back is for *our* places," said {{brother}}.

"I think those are magic too," said {{hero}}.
