# Sharp and Steady, Part One

The route north of Saffron had no shortage of trainers.

It was one of the more traveled roads in Kanto — connecting major cities, used by trainers at every level heading in both directions. Which meant battles were available the way water was available near a river. Constantly, and without much searching.

Koda decided to use every one of them.

Not for badges. Not for a destination.

Just sharpening.

---

## Morning — Kadabra

The first trainer appeared before the city was fully out of sight behind them — a boy with a Scyther and a confident stance that said he'd won enough battles to expect winning.

Koda sent Kadabra.

Scyther was fast — genuinely fast, the kind that made most opponents reactive before they'd formed a thought. It opened with Slash, closing distance in a single movement.

Kadabra teleported.

Scyther adjusted mid-swing — better reflexes than expected — and caught Kadabra's new position on the follow-through.

Kadabra took the hit and didn't teleport away from it. Held the position. Fired Psybeam at contact range.

The exchange was close and immediate, none of the distance management Kadabra usually preferred. Koda had been deliberate about this — Kadabra's tendency to create separation was good strategy but it was also a habit. Habits became predictable.

*Learn to fight in the space you don't prefer.*

Scyther pressed again. Kadabra met it again — Disable landing on the third exchange, locking Scyther's Slash, and then the methodical Psybeam work that Kadabra did better than almost anything finishing the battle cleanly.

The boy recalled Scyther and nodded with genuine respect.

"Your Kadabra doesn't run," he said.

"It used to," Koda said. "It's learning."

---

## Midmorning — Pidgeot

A trainer with a Magneton and an Electrode — specifically chosen to challenge flying types, she said cheerfully, like she'd been waiting for a Pidgeot to come along.

She wasn't wrong. Magneton's Thunder disrupted Pidgeot's aerial mobility, forcing lower altitude. Electrode's speed on the ground cut off landing options.

Pidgeot's first two passes came up short — disrupted, redirected, unable to build the momentum it liked.

Koda watched without calling adjustments.

*Find it yourself. You know what to do when you stop trying to do what you always do.*

Pidgeot climbed instead of diving — up and above the Thunder's effective range, using the altitude not for speed but for positioning. From above, the geometry of Magneton and Electrode's coverage had gaps.

It found one.

Steel Wing through the gap, Magneton down.

Electrode tried to follow up immediately but Pidgeot was already climbing again — patient now, circling, waiting for the next gap to appear rather than forcing one.

It appeared.

Second Steel Wing landed clean.

The trainer recalled both Pokémon and looked at the sky where Pidgeot was still circling, unhurried.

"It changed strategy mid-battle," she said.

"It's been doing that more," Koda said. "I'm trying to get out of its way enough to let it."
