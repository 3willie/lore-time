# The Saffron Challenge, Part Two

## Alakazam

Kadabra emerged and felt Alakazam immediately.

Not fear. Recognition. The way a younger musician feels standing next to someone who has played for thirty years — aware of the distance between them, but not broken by it.

Alakazam hadn't moved. Both spoons held level, eyes half closed, the psychic presence in the chamber steady and total.

Sabrina stood exactly as she had at the beginning. Watching Koda more than the battlefield.

"Your Kadabra is well trained," she said. "It won't be enough."

"Maybe not," Koda said. "We'll find out together."

---

Alakazam opened with Future Sight — not an immediate attack, a delayed strike placed precisely in time, building somewhere ahead of the present moment like a storm system scheduled to arrive.

*It's not just reading the present. It's setting conditions on the future.*

Koda felt the weight of it — an attack that would land regardless of what happened in the meantime, timed perfectly to hit when Kadabra was most vulnerable.

*So we have to end this before the future it planned arrives.*

"Kadabra — Psybeam, sustained pressure. Don't stop."

Kadabra pushed forward — not cautiously, continuously, Psybeam pouring out in a steady focused stream that forced Alakazam to respond rather than dictate.

Alakazam deflected with Psychic — not blocking exactly, redirecting, bending the Psybeam sideways with casual precision.

It hadn't moved its feet.

Kadabra teleported — reappeared close, trying to get inside Alakazam's preferred range.

Alakazam was already facing the new position.

It had known.

Psychic hit Kadabra hard — lifting it, holding it suspended for one long moment, then setting it down with controlled force. Not cruel. Just definitive.

Kadabra staggered. Stayed up.

*Good. Stay up.*

"Kadabra — Disable."

Kadabra's eyes locked onto Alakazam and the Disable pulse went out — targeting the Future Sight, trying to interrupt the building strike before it landed.

Alakazam's eyes opened fully for the first time.

*That surprised it.*

The Future Sight flickered — disrupted but not fully cancelled. Weakened.

"Now — everything. Psybeam."

Kadabra pulled from whatever reserves it had left and released the strongest Psybeam Koda had seen from it — focused, furious, the kind of output that came from a Pokémon that had decided this was the moment.

Alakazam raised both spoons.

The attacks met in the center of the chamber.

The collision pushed air outward in a hard ring — Koda felt it across his whole body, the pressure wave of two psychic forces meeting at full output.

For three seconds neither moved.

Then Kadabra's beam broke.

The feedback hit Kadabra and it went down — not slowly, all at once, the way things fall when they've given everything and there's simply nothing left to give.

The weakened Future Sight landed a half second later.

Unnecessary. But it landed anyway.

Koda recalled Kadabra.

He held the ball for a moment.

*You pushed further than I've ever seen you push. That was yours.*

---

One Pokémon left each.

Koda looked at his belt.

Arcanine was tired from the Mr. Mime battle. Pidgeot hadn't been built for this chamber or this matchup. Dragonair had handled Sabrina's Kadabra well but Dragon type against a pure Psychic this strong —

He reached for Arcanine's ball.

Arcanine stepped out and looked at Alakazam without flinching.

Alakazam regarded Arcanine with the same total attention it had given everything else.

Sabrina spoke for the first time since the battle began in earnest.

"Fire type against Psychic. Unconventional."

"Alakazam is a thinking Pokémon," Koda said. "Arcanine is a feeling one. Sometimes feeling moves faster than thinking."

Sabrina looked at him with something that finally shifted in her expression. Not quite surprise. Closer to interest.

---

Alakazam opened with Psychic immediately — full force, no preamble.

"Arcanine — don't plan. Just move."

Arcanine launched sideways on pure instinct, the Psychic grazing his flank rather than landing clean. He kept moving — not in a pattern, not in a calculated arc, just continuous motion driven by instinct and the deep trust Koda had built into him over months.

Alakazam tracked him. Adjusted. Fired again.

Arcanine wasn't where it aimed.

*It's working. Alakazam reads intention — Arcanine isn't forming intentions fast enough to read.*

"Flamethrower — when it feels right. Don't wait for me."

He gave Arcanine the battle.

Arcanine ran a wide arc and released Flamethrower not when Koda would have called it but when his own instinct said now — and the timing was different from anything calculated, catching Alakazam mid-adjustment, landing across its left side.

Alakazam stumbled. First time it had moved its feet.

It responded with Thunder Punch — faster than its previous attacks, abandoning the psychic approach for something more direct.

It connected. Arcanine skidded hard across the chamber floor, electricity crawling through his coat.

He got up.

Koda said nothing.

Arcanine shook the static from his fur and looked at Alakazam.

Then he did something Koda hadn't seen before — he went completely still. All that continuous motion just — stopped.

Alakazam read the stillness. Prepared.

Arcanine moved.

Not Extreme Speed — something between speed and intention, a full commitment that had no calculation in it whatsoever.

Flamethrower released at point blank range before Alakazam could process that the stillness had been the move.

The hit was decisive.

Alakazam stood for a moment — both spoons lowering slowly — and then sat down with a kind of dignity that only very old and very powerful Pokémon managed even in defeat.

The chamber went quiet.

---

Sabrina stood still for a long moment.

She walked across the chamber floor toward Koda — unhurried, the way she did everything — and stopped in front of him.

She looked at Arcanine first. Then at Koda.

"You gave your Arcanine the battle," she said.

"It knew what to do better than I did."

"That's not something most trainers learn." A pause. "Some never do."

She held out the Marsh Badge — deep green, understated, smaller than he expected.

Koda took it.

He looked at it once — then looked at Arcanine, who was still breathing hard, fur singed along one side, standing with the particular exhaustion of a Pokémon that had spent everything on something that mattered.

Koda crossed the chamber and put both hands on Arcanine's face.

"That was perfect," he said quietly. "Every bit of it."

Arcanine pressed into his hands and exhaled — long and slow, like setting something down.

---

Sabrina spoke from behind him.

"There's another trainer. Your companion. She's outside."

Koda turned.

"I know," Sabrina said, answering his unasked question. "I sensed her the moment you both arrived in the city. Her ability is genuine." A pause. "She should come in."

Koda looked at the gym entrance.

Then back at Sabrina.

"She'll want to earn it," he said.

"I know that too," Sabrina said.

And for the first time — small, brief, genuine — she smiled.
