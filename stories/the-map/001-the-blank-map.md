# The Blank Map

The attic smelled like dust and old summers.

{{dad}} was looking for the box of winter coats. {{hero}} and {{brother}} were supposed to be helping. Instead, they had found a long wooden tube tucked behind the water heater, sealed with a cork.

"What's this?" {{brother}} asked, already pulling the cork out.

Inside was a roll of thick, creamy paper. They spread it out on the floor.

It was a map. Or it was *trying* to be a map. Most of it was completely blank. But right in the middle, drawn in tiny brown ink, was a house. *Their* house — with the crooked gate, and the apple tree, and the attic window with a little round top.

"Huh," said {{dad}}, kneeling down beside them. His voice went soft. "I haven't seen this since I was your age. It was Grandpa's."

"Why is it empty?" {{hero}} asked.

{{dad}} smiled a strange, faraway smile. "Grandpa used to say it isn't empty. It's *waiting*. He said the map draws what you earn."

"Earn how?"

"He never told me." {{dad}} rolled it up carefully and handed it to {{hero}}. "Maybe you two can find out."

That afternoon, the boys forgot about the map. They were busy. Their neighbor, Mrs. Okafor, had dropped a whole bag of groceries on her front steps, and oranges were rolling everywhere — into the hedge, under the car, down toward the drain.

{{brother}} didn't even think about it. He just ran. He chased every single orange, even the one that had rolled all the way to the corner, and brought them back in his shirt like a basket.

"You," said Mrs. Okafor, "are a very good boy."

That night, {{hero}} woke up to a sound. A tiny scratching, like a pen on paper.

He crept to the desk, where they'd left the map. In the moonlight, he watched brown ink appear by itself — curling out from the edge of their house, drawing a path, a hill, and then a lake. A deep round lake with something hidden underneath.

Tiny letters wrote themselves along the shore: *The Library Under the Lake.*

{{hero}} shook his brother awake.

"{{brother}}," he whispered. "I think you earned something."
