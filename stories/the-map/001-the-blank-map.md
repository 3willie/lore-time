# The Blank Map

The attic was dusty. *Achoo!*

{{dad}} was looking for the winter coats. {{hero}} and {{brother}} were *supposed* to be helping. But they found something better.

A long wooden tube, hiding behind a box.

Inside was a roll of paper. They spread it out on the floor.

It was a map! But it was almost empty. There was only one thing on it, right in the middle: a tiny drawing of a house.

*Their* house. With the crooked gate, and the apple tree, and the little round attic window.

"Wow," said {{dad}} softly. "That was Grandpa's map. I haven't seen it since I was little."

"Why is it empty?" asked {{hero}}.

"Grandpa used to say it's not empty. It's *waiting*," said {{dad}}. "He said when you do something kind, the map draws something new."

That afternoon, they forgot all about the map.

Their neighbor, Mrs. Okafor, dropped her bag of groceries. Oranges rolled *everywhere*! Under the car. Into the bushes. Down the sidewalk.

{{brother}} didn't even stop to think. He ran and ran, and picked up every single orange. He carried them back in his shirt, like a basket.

"Thank you!" said Mrs. Okafor. "What a kind boy."

That night, {{hero}} heard a sound. *Scritch, scratch.* Like a pencil on paper.

He tiptoed to the desk. In the moonlight, he saw the map… drawing all by itself!

A little path. A hill. And a round, blue lake.

Tiny letters appeared: *The Library Under the Lake.*

{{hero}} shook his brother awake.

"{{brother}}!" he whispered. "Your kindness made a new place!"
