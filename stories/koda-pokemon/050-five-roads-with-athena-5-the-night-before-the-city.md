# Five Roads With Athena 5: The Night Before the City

They camped on a low hill one hour south of the next city, close enough to see its lights spreading across the dark below them.

All the Pokémon were out. The full team — Arcanine, Kadabra, Pidgeot, Dragonair, and Eevee moving between them all like it had always been part of the group.

Koda sat with his back against a flat rock, watching the city lights.

Athena was cross-legged nearby, Eevee in her lap, making notes in a small journal. She wrote the way she moved — thoughtful, unhurried, like the words would wait for her.

"What are you writing?" Koda asked.

"Everything the Pokémon told me today," she said. "Not in words. Just — impressions. What they were feeling. What the land felt like to them."

"The Tauros herd. The Rattata."

"All of it." She turned a page. "If I don't write it down it gets mixed up with what I think I know."

Koda looked at his own team.

Dragonair was at the hill's edge, facing the city lights, its pearl orb reflecting them faintly. Still and attentive, the way it got when something was ahead worth paying attention to.

"It knows we're close to something," Koda said.

"They all do," Athena said. "Arcanine's been restless since this afternoon. Kadabra too."

Koda had noticed.

He looked at the lights below.

Another gym ahead. Another badge. Whatever Team Rocket was building toward in the background. Meadow somewhere on her own path, Wren back home probably driving everyone around him gently crazy.

All of it moving forward whether he hurried or not.

"What's your plan after the next badge?" he asked.

Athena was quiet for a moment. "There's something happening with wild Pokémon two routes east. Unusual behavior. I want to look at it."

"Team Rocket?"

"Maybe." She closed her journal. "Someone's disrupting them. The patterns are wrong for natural causes."

Koda filed that. Didn't push further.

Dragonair turned from the city lights and glided back to his side.

He put a hand on its neck — that quiet gesture that had started on a limestone shelf beside a river pool and hadn't needed to change since.

"Good journey this week," he said.

Athena looked out at the lights.

"Yeah," she said. "It was."

The fire held between them and the city waited below, patient as everything eventually was.
