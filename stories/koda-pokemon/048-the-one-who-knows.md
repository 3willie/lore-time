# The One Who Knows

Koda didn't call for her out loud. He never did.

He was three days out from Lavender Town, camped alone on a hillside while the rest of his team slept, and something had gone quietly wrong inside him in a way he couldn't name. Not an injury. Not a battle lost. Just — the particular kind of heavy that accumulates when you've been brave for too long without rest.

He sat with his knees pulled to his chest and looked at the dark and didn't say anything to anyone.

---

Karen was three mountain ranges away.

She'd been tracking a rumored Nidoqueen migration through the northern highlands for a week, notebook full, boots muddy, completely in her element. She was good at this — reading terrain, following signs, staying patient. Koda had gotten his stillness from her.

She was crouched behind a boulder studying tracks in the soft earth when she felt it.

Nothing she could explain. Just a shift — the way a room changes when someone you love walks in. Except there was no room, and no one there.

She straightened slowly and looked at the sky.

A small shape drifted down from the clouds. Pale pink, barely there, eyes ancient and playful and bottomless all at once.

Mew hovered in front of her and said nothing.

Karen looked at it for a long moment. She'd been on expeditions her whole life. She'd seen rare Pokémon, strange phenomena, things that didn't make it into any official report.

She had never seen Mew.

"My son," she said. Not a question.

Mew turned and drifted upward.

She closed her notebook and followed.

---

Koda heard her boots on the hillside before he saw her. That specific sound — unhurried, steady — unwound something in his shoulders before he even turned around.

Karen sat down beside him without asking what was wrong. She didn't reach for him immediately. She just settled into the dark next to him, shoulder touching his, and looked at the same patch of nothing he'd been looking at.

After a while she said, "The stars are better out here than at home."

"Yeah," Koda said. His voice came out smaller than he meant it to.

She put her arm around him then, and he leaned into her the way he hadn't since he was small — completely, without holding anything back.

They sat like that for a long time.

She didn't ask him to explain. She didn't tell him it would pass. She just stayed, warm and solid and entirely unhurried, while the night moved around them and his team slept on and the distant trees rustled with whatever small lives lived inside them.

At the edge of the firelight, barely visible, a small pink shape drifted in a slow, lazy circle — then dissolved into the dark like it had never been there at all.

---

Later, when the heaviness had gone somewhere smaller and more manageable, Karen poured two cups of tea from her thermos and handed him one.

"I was tracking a Nidoqueen migration," she said.

"Sorry," Koda said.

"I'm not." She sipped her tea. "She'll still be there tomorrow."

Koda looked down at his cup. "How did you—"

"I just knew." She said it simply, the way she said most true things. "I always know."

He didn't ask anything else.

Some things didn't need explaining. They just needed to be true.
